## Report

On a record whose closing section has a long part heading, the jump strip under the banner cuts
the part's count out of its own chip. The number is not shortened and not ellipsised — it is
simply not painted, and nothing on the chip says a number is missing.

Found by the clipping gate, which walks every record of every project:

    node scripts/check-clipping.mjs
    CLIPPED 1600  /p/agent-monitoring/work/WORK-0012  a.res-jump-link cuts span.res-jump-count by 50.3px — "8"
    CLIPPED 1152  /p/agent-monitoring/work/WORK-0012  a.res-jump-link cuts span.res-jump-count by 50.3px — "8"
    CLIPPED 960   /p/agent-monitoring/work/WORK-0012  a.res-jump-link cuts span.res-jump-count by 50.3px — "8"

Reproduce: `npm run dev`, open `/p/agent-monitoring/work/WORK-0012` and look at the strip of
chips at the top of the Outcome. The first chip reads "What is on the screen that was not"
against a chip capped at 240px; the "8" that belongs on its right-hand end is nowhere.

Cause: `.res-jump-link` is `max-width: 240px; overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis`, and the
label is bare text inside it with the count as a trailing child element. `text-overflow` truncates
the *text*, so the text is the thing that fits and the count is the thing pushed past the box —
where the chip's own `overflow: hidden` guillotines it. The one part of the chip that must never
be cut is the only part that was.

Worth noting how this arrived: no code changed under it. The strip has been correct on every
record in the vault until one was written whose Outcome has a part heading longer than about
thirty characters. The gate found it on content, not on a diff, which is the argument for walking
every record of every project rather than a sample.

Severity low: the same count is printed beside the same part in the contents rail on the left of
the record, so nothing is unreachable, and the chip still says what it links to and still links
there. It is on this board because a number that is silently amputated is the exact defect class
BUG-0006 was filed for, and the fix is three lines.

## Resolution

**Root cause.** `text-overflow: ellipsis` truncates a box's *inline text*; it knows nothing about
a sibling element that comes after that text. `.res-jump-link` mixed the two — a bare text label
plus a `<span>` count — inside one 240px box with `overflow: hidden`, so under pressure the text
was allowed to fill the box and the count was pushed past its edge and clipped away. The chip
gave ground with the wrong thing.

**Fix,** in `src/components/RecordBody.tsx` and `src/styles/app.css`:

- the label is now its own element, `.res-jump-text`, with `min-width: 0; overflow: hidden;
  text-overflow: ellipsis` — so it is the thing that shortens, visibly, with an ellipsis the
  reader can see;
- `.res-jump-count` and the evidence check-mark are `flex: none`, so neither can be squeezed or
  pushed out;
- `text-overflow` comes off the chip itself (it has nothing left to do there) and `min-width: 0`
  goes on, so the chip can shrink inside a wrapping row without its children refusing to.

This is the same shape of fix as vault BUG-0006 on the dashboard's Agents table and BUG-0004 on
the bug board: name the one element that is allowed to lose characters, carry `min-width: 0` all
the way down to it, and pin everything that is a number.

**Verified.** `node scripts/check-clipping.mjs` — 53 screens (44 records across both projects) ×
7 widths, 371 screen loads: "clean: nothing is cut without an ellipsis, nothing is painted over
anything". The three findings quoted in the report are gone, and the chip that produced them now
reads "What is on the screen that was not… 8" at 1152px with the count in place. `npm run build`
and `npm run smoke` are clean on the same build.

## For humans

Near the top of one record's last section sits a row of small link chips. Each names a part further down the page and carries that part's count at its right-hand end. On the page of `WORK-0012`, one of this project's own work records, the first chip read "What is on the screen that was not". The 8 that belonged beside it was simply not drawn. Nothing on the chip said a number was missing.

**The rule that shortens the words knows nothing about the number.** The chip, `.res-jump-link`, is capped at 240 pixels wide. Its `text-overflow: ellipsis` trims overrunning text and marks the cut with "…" — text only. The count sat beside the text as a separate piece, so the words filled the box and the number was pushed past the edge. Out there the chip's own `overflow: hidden` throws away whatever lands outside it.

It is like a shelf that shortens a long book to make room, then shoves the bookend off the end instead.

**No code changed underneath it.** The strip was right on every record until somebody wrote a part heading longer than about thirty characters. Content did this, not an edit.

**The fix is three lines: give the words their own box, pin the number.** The words now sit in `.res-jump-text`, the only piece allowed to lose characters, and it shows the "…" while it loses them. The count and the check-mark beside it are `flex: none`, so neither can be squeezed or pushed out.

**What says the screens are clean is `node scripts/check-clipping.mjs`.** That script opens every record page of both projects at seven window widths. It reports anything cut without a visible "…", which is what keeps a reader from trusting a number that has quietly gone. It caught this chip at three of those widths. Afterwards: 371 page loads, nothing cut, and that chip ends in its 8 again.

**Nobody was ever stuck.** The same count is printed beside the same part in the list down the left of the record. One warning about this page's clock: its three times fall within two seconds. They mark when these words were written, not when the fault was found.

A number that disappears without a mark is worse than one too long to fit.
