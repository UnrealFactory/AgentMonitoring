## Report

The dashboard's two burn-up charts label their x axis with clock times and no date whenever the
bucket is smaller than a day, so a chart covering a week says nothing about which week.

Reproduce, with the vault as it stands:

1. `npm run dev`, open `/p/relay?range=7d`. The axis reads
   `12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00`. Seven days of data, five labels, no date on any of
   them.
2. Open `/p/agent-monitoring` — a project four days old, so this is its **default** All-time
   view. The axis reads `06:00 · 00:00 · 18:00 · 12:00 · 06:00 · 00:00 · 18:00`: not only
   dateless but *descending*, which reads as a broken axis rather than as three days of
   six-hour buckets.

Expected: the axis says which period the chart covers. play.grafana.org, set to Last 7 days,
prints `08/13 00:00 … 08/19 00:00` — the date on every tick.

Cause: `timeAxis` in `src/lib/dashboard.ts` derives the label format from the bucket **step**
(`granularity: step < DAY ? "hour" : "day"`) when it has to come from the **span**. A seven-day
chart bucketed in twelve-hour steps is still a seven-day chart. The descending sequence is a
second, related defect in the same place: the labelled buckets are chosen by striding backwards
from the *last* bucket, so they land wherever that stride happens to fall (18:00, 12:00, 06:00)
instead of on a clock boundary.

Severity medium: every number on the chart is correct and the tooltip names the bucket in full,
so nothing here is wrong — it is unreadable, on the one screen whose job is to be read at a
glance, and it loses the pairing against the Grafana bar on axis honesty.

Found by the P4 design critic in round 1's blind side-by-side against play.grafana.org
(progress/rounds.jsonl, P4 round 1, verdict fail).

## Resolution

**Root cause.** Two decisions in `timeAxis`/`BurnUp`, both of which threw away the one thing an
axis exists to say.

The label *format* came from the bucket step — `granularity: step < DAY ? "hour" : "day"` — so
any chart whose buckets were sub-daily printed clock times, no matter how many days it spanned.
A week bucketed in twelve-hour steps is a week; the step is an implementation detail of the
drawing, not a description of the period.

The labelled buckets were then chosen by *striding by index* from the last bucket backwards
(`labelIndices`), so they landed wherever the arithmetic put them. On a four-day project with
six-hour buckets that produced `06:00 · 00:00 · 18:00 · 12:00 · 06:00` — each label three
buckets after the one before it, and therefore in descending clock order, which is exactly what
a broken axis looks like.

**Fix.** Ticks are now chosen the way a clock is read (`axisTicks` in `src/lib/dashboard.ts`):

- the interval is the smallest **natural** one — 1/2/3/6/12 hours, 1/2/3/7/14/28 days — that is a
  whole number of buckets *and* leaves at least 64px between labels at the plot's real pixel
  width;
- a tick may only sit on a boundary of that interval (`bucket.start % interval === 0`), so
  sub-daily ticks fall on 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 … and never on 18:00, 12:00, 06:00;
- whenever the interval is under a day, every tick that opens a new UTC day prints that date on
  a second line beneath it, and the plot's height grows by 12px so the second line is inside the
  card rather than clipped by it.

A window shorter than one natural interval (a two-hour-old project) has nothing aligned inside
it, so it falls back to labelling its two ends rather than drawing an axis with no ticks at all.

`granularity` survives, with its meaning narrowed and documented: it describes what one
*bucket* covers, which is what the tooltip needs ("18 Aug, 12:00 – 18:00" against "Tue 12 Aug"),
and it is no longer what the ticks are chosen from.

**Verified** against six shapes of window, run through the built module rather than reasoned
about (`timeAxis` + `axisTicks` at a 600px plot):

    2h-old project      13:00/18 Aug · 14:00 · 15:00 · … · 22:00
    4-day project       00:00/15 Aug · 12:00 · 00:00/16 Aug · 12:00 · 00:00/17 Aug · …
    7 days              12 Aug · 13 Aug · 14 Aug · 15 Aug · 16 Aug · 17 Aug · 18 Aug
    22 days (all time)  30 Jul · 2 Aug · 5 Aug · 8 Aug · 11 Aug · 14 Aug · 17 Aug
    90 days             21 May · 4 Jun · 18 Jun · 2 Jul · 16 Jul · 30 Jul · 13 Aug

The two cases in the report are the second and third lines: the four-day project's descending
clock times are now ascending days with the time under them, and the seven-day view is dated on
every tick, which is what play.grafana.org prints at Last 7 days.

`scripts/check-urlstate.mjs` now asserts it, so it cannot come back quietly: at `?range=7d` at
least one tick must match a date (`/^\d{1,2} [A-Z][a-z]{2}$/`). `npm run check:clipping` is clean
over 50 screens × 7 widths with the taller axis band, and `npm run build` is clean.

## For humans

The dashboard draws two charts of a project's progress. Set one to the last seven days, `?range=7d`, and the labels underneath read `12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00`. Seven days of work, five labels, and not one of them says which day.

**On `/p/agent-monitoring`, this app's own dashboard, it was worse.** The times ran downwards — 06:00, 00:00, 18:00, 12:00 — which reads like a broken chart, not three days. The P4 design critic found it comparing our chart with `play.grafana.org`, the public demo of a widely used charting tool, which dates every label of the same span.

**Nothing on the chart was wrong; it simply could not be read.** Every number was right, and pointing at a bar still named its period in full.

**The labels were chosen from the width of one bar, not the length of the chart.** The chart cuts its span into equal pieces — a week into half-days, a four-day project into six-hour pieces. The code behind them, in `src/lib/dashboard.ts`, printed clock times whenever a piece was shorter than a day, however many days the chart covered.

It is like writing the time on a holiday photograph and never the date.

**The labels also landed wherever the counting stopped.** They were picked by stepping backwards from the last piece a fixed number at a time, so they fell on whatever hour the arithmetic reached. That is why they ran downwards.

**Labels now sit where a clock would put them.** A new step chooses the gap: the smallest of a short list of ordinary intervals, an hour up to twenty-eight days, that divides the bars evenly and leaves 64 pixels between labels.

**A label may only fall on a round boundary of that interval**, so hours land on 00:00, 06:00, 12:00. When the gap is under a day, every label that opens a new day prints that date underneath, and the chart grew 12 pixels taller so that second line is not cut off.

**A chart younger than one whole interval has no round boundary inside it**, so a two-hour-old one labels its two ends rather than drawing no labels at all.

**I ran six shapes of window through the built code.** The seven-day view now reads `12 Aug · 13 Aug · 14 Aug` and on to the 18th, and the four-day project reads as ascending days with the clock time under them.

**One new demand stops this coming back quietly**: `npm run check:urlstate`, which drives the real screens, now insists on at least one dated label in the seven-day view.

An axis that does not say when is a picture, not a measurement.
