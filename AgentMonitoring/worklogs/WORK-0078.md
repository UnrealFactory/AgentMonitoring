## What

Rework the human area's picture rule from one summary diagram per record to one scene per
beat, drawn inside the beat between the bold lead-in and the paragraph: `docs/HUMAN_STYLE.md`,
the renderer that reads a retelling back (`src/lib/human.ts`, `src/components/HumanView.tsx`),
and the three live records that carry pictures.

## Why

The owner approved a rendered mock of the eli5 shape — picture above, words below, one per
beat. A single diagram at the end of a record is a picture of the record rather than of a
step, and it arrives after the words it was meant to prepare the reader for.

## How

Read the approved mock and its generator as ground truth for the icon vocabulary and the
compositions; re-author them in English at the contract's grid; extend the renderer without
moving any existing behaviour; rewrite the three records through the CLI only.

## For humans

A few of the stories this project keeps about itself carry a drawing. Until today each of
those had one picture, near the end, trying to hold the whole story at once. Now every step
of the story that earns a picture has its own, sitting above that step's words.

**A picture now belongs to one step.** The rules for writing these plain-language pages
live in one file, `docs/HUMAN_STYLE.md`, and every worker here is handed them before it
writes one. They used to ask for a single drawing per story. They now ask for a small scene
per step: the things that step talks about drawn as plain shapes — a page for a file, a box
with one lit dot for the program that hands files out, an eye for the part that notices
when you save — and every link between them as an arrow with a word on it. The picture goes
under the step's heading and above its paragraph, so you meet the step twice: once as a
picture, once in words.

**A step with nothing honest to draw keeps its words and gets no picture.** Every shape,
arrow, word and number in a drawing has to be a sentence of the story it sits in. The
sketch this was approved from showed somebody restarting a program twice; the story it came
from says only that we blamed an old copy of that program twice, and never that anyone
restarted anything, so that part is not in the drawing that shipped.

**The words inside a picture now have rows of their own.** The shapes on one row, the names
of those shapes on the next, the closing line alone at the foot. Two names that just miss
each other on my screen land on top of each other on a machine whose lettering is wider,
and nothing in the app can see inside a picture to warn you — a picture is a document of
its own.

It is like proofreading a poster through the window of the shop it is hanging in.

**So a script measures them instead.** It opens each drawing in a real browser, measures
the box around every word, and fails on any overlap, anything sitting too near the edge,
and any lettering that would come back too small to read in the narrowest column a story
page ever gives a picture. Then it does the whole thing again in a deliberately wider
lettering, standing in for the machines I do not have. All ten drawings pass both times. A
second script walked the window from 700 pixels wide to 1920 and reported that narrowest
column: 424 pixels, at a window of 1104.

**Ten drawings replaced three.** `BUG-0025`, the day saving a file stopped changing the
app, has six, one per step. `BUG-0004`, where a stored number came out of a page as
something else, has one. `WORK-0013`, the week every screen started sharing one count of
changes, has three. The other steps of those three stories kept their words and took no
picture.

**Nothing else in those three stories moved.** A third script strips the picture lines out
of the old and the new and compares everything left, word for word: 445, 533 and 761 words,
the same. I then opened all three in the app at two window widths and looked at every step.

A picture nobody has measured is a drawing you are hoping about.
