## What

Localise every word the app itself prints into Korean, keep English as the second language,
and make the choice a control in the sidebar footer that applies instantly and is
remembered. Records stay exactly as their authors wrote them.

## Why

A Korean user reported the screens as unusable-in-place: the chart subtitle "Started against
done, running total, with the change over the last 7 days" and all other UI chrome are
English and inconvenient — make the UI Korean.

## How

A hand-rolled i18n module (no dependency) in src/lib/i18n: en.ts is the key set, ko.ts is
typed against it so a missing translation is a compile error, and t() reads a module-level
locale so lib/words.ts, lib/format.ts and lib/dashboard.ts — none of them components — can
call it. AppProvider subscribes once at the root, so a change repaints the whole window.

lib/words.ts keeps its job as the app's vocabulary and now reads its words from the
dictionaries, so the one-word-per-state law holds in both languages. Dates, durations and
relative times are written twice from UTC parts rather than handed to a locale formatter.

Pretendard Variable is bundled beside Inter (metric-compatible, Hangul only, dynamic
subset, no CDN), headings and the app's own sentences get word-break: keep-all.

## Outcome

**Shipped.** The app opens in Korean. Every word it prints itself — nav, tabs and their
counts, filters and their menus, group headings, chart titles, subtitles, legends, axis
labels and tooltips, the NOW strip, feed verbs, meta labels, relative times, durations,
absolute dates, empty states, error states, onboarding, toasts, context menus, the command
palette, the keyboard legend, aria-labels and the window title — comes from
`src/lib/i18n/{en,ko}.ts` through a small `t()`. 531 keys, 140 of them functions with real
parameters the compiler checks; `ko.ts` is typed as `Dict`, so a key added without a Korean
twin does not build. No i18n dependency.

The chart subtitle the user quoted now reads **시작 대비 완료 누적 추이 · 최근 7일 변화 포함**.

**Records are untouched.** Titles, What/Why/How, notes, comments, resolutions, agent
handles, tags and ids render exactly as their authors wrote them — the renderer's P6 rule
did not move.

**One word per state, in Korean.** work 진행 중 / 완료 / 중단; bug 열림 / 진행 중 / 해결됨 /
닫힘; the set of the two that still need somebody is 미해결, never one of its members'
names. 담당자 없음 for an empty assignee, 치명적 / 높음 / 보통 / 낮음 for severity. Decided
once in the header of ko.ts, including why `open` is 열림 there and not what the brief
suggested. Counts keep their denominator: 12개 중 2개 진행 중.

**The toggle** is a two-segment control in the sidebar footer, each option named in its own
language. It applies to the whole window on click, and is remembered: localStorage in
browser mode, `settings.json` beside the vault path in the desktop app (two new Tauri
commands; the settings file is now read-modify-written so the two choices cannot clobber
each other).

**Type.** Pretendard Variable is bundled from npm and sits behind Inter in one stack —
Inter keeps every Latin glyph, Pretendard carries the Hangul it has no glyph for, and the
two are metric-compatible so a row does not change height with the language. The dynamic
subset means a window fetches only the syllables it paints: 10 files of about 35kB on the
dashboard, and no `https://` anywhere in the built CSS. Headings and the app's own
sentences take `word-break: keep-all`, so 이 페이지의 no longer breaks after 이 페.

## For humans

A Korean user said the screens were unusable where they sat. Everything the app puts around the records was English. They quoted the line under one chart: "Started against done, running total, with the change over the last 7 days".

**The app now speaks Korean.** Every word the app prints itself: tabs, filters, chart labels, dates, empty screens, error messages, menus. That chart line now reads 시작 대비 완료 누적 추이 · 최근 7일 변화 포함. English is the second language, picked in a two-part switch at the foot of the sidebar, and remembered.

`Pretendard Variable`, the Korean typeface, ships inside the app beside `Inter`, cut to the same measurements so a row does not change height when you switch.

**Every phrase lives twice, once in each language.** There are 531 of them, in `src/lib/i18n/en.ts` and its Korean twin. The Korean list is checked against the English one when the app is built, so a phrase added without a Korean twin refuses to build.

It is like a form printed in two columns: leave a cell empty and the printer will not take the job.

What the agents wrote — titles, the body of every record, ids, handles, tags — is untouched.

**Korean was decided, not translated.** The app allows one word per state, and the instructions for this work suggested 미해결 for a bug that is merely open. But 미해결 names the set that still needs somebody: the open ones and the ones being worked on. Giving a set's name to one of its members is the mistake a reviewer caught in English: a tab saying Open 5 above a group saying Open 3.

So an open bug is 열림, and the reason sits at the top of the Korean list.

**Word order is a layout problem, not a wording one.** A line in the activity list reads "nova started WORK-0012" in English and "nova WORK-0012 시작" in Korean, so the verb and the id trade places and that row had to be rebuilt rather than retranslated.

Two more places moved for the same reason. The busiest-hour figure keeps a slot of its own with the sentence split around it, because the number comes before its noun in one language and after it in the other. And the column naming who holds a bug measures 담당자 없음 — nobody has this one — as five wide letters rather than five narrow ones, or the board would have cut its own vocabulary in half.

**`npm run check:i18n` opens 21 screens in Korean and fails on Latin words.** It is new, and ids, agent handles, tags, paths and commands are excepted. It comes back clean, and pointed at the English build it counts 3,182 such words, which is what makes the zero worth anything.

**Every other check reads both languages now.** The ones that drive the app and compare what is on screen knew only English words. Each takes the language as an argument and reads what it expects from the same two lists the window reads, so both are walked instead of one being tested and the other assumed. The check that hunts for text cut off or painted over crossed 74 screens at seven widths in each language, and came back clean.

**Only the app is bilingual.** The command-line tool and the agents' manual stay English, and so do the headings a record is written with: a Korean screen calls them 한 일 / 이유 / 방법 but still tells you `## What` when one is missing.

Deciding the words is the work; translating them is only the typing.
