## What

The App feedback board's human half was an undesigned screen the moment more than one item
lacked a retelling — and it was the half the board opened on. `items.some(hasHumanArea)`
made one retold row of five decide the opening view for all of them, and the page then drew
four copies of the detail screen's `.human-empty` box: the same headline, the same
paragraph, four commands differing in seven characters, four Copy buttons and four "read the
agent's half" buttons. The list grew 640px → 1631px and the instruction outweighed the five
titles it hung off about five to one.

Round 2 splits that box by owner and fixes two things behind it:
- `BoardHumanNotice` (src/components/HumanView.tsx) — one board-level notice carrying the
  what, the whose, the command (with the topmost missing row's real id) and the style
  contract. A row with no retelling keeps one quiet line, `.feedback-none`.
- The board's default and its `is-thin` marker now read different questions: the default
  reads the majority (ties fall to the agent half, the half every item is guaranteed to
  have); `is-thin` reads the *none* case; and a mixed board prints the count on the segment
  before it is pressed, the way the tabs beside it print theirs.
- The correction line is on **both** halves of a work log and a bug. `work update --message`
  and `bug comment` post a correction without rewriting the retelling, so hiding the line on
  the human half showed a reader who cannot read the agent area a story the record itself
  says is wrong, with nothing admitting it. On that half it names which half the trail is on
  and carries the reader across (`flushSync` then `scrollIntoView`, because the anchor is not
  mounted until the swap draws).

## Why

"Empty states designed, not blank" is the Design system's own line, and a designed state
repeated N times is boilerplate, not a designed state — the board stopped being scannable as
a board. This is one record from live: `~/.AgentMonitoring/feedback` held exactly FB-0001
with no human area, every new item the CLI accepts requires `--human`, and SPEC puts the
mixed board in writing, so the first feedback any agent filed would open the owner's own
board into it.

## How

`.human-notice` in src/styles/app.css: 720px — short of the 860px rows, with the same 2px
`--accent` inset edge the retelling's closing line carries, so it reads as the board's own
voice rather than one more indented row. Both its sentences keep `--human-measure`. No new
tokens; no colour literal in the whole diff.

Gates: `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` clean. `check-keys` clean — 271 on live, and
17 of those are the toggle's, including three new ones that lock the correction line onto
both halves and follow it across. `check-clipping` clean at 8 widths in both languages on
live (928 screen loads each). `check-errors` clean, 7 failures × 2 languages. `check-live`
clean, 19. `check-i18n` en clean; ko reports 39 findings, all on `a.ref-inline` inside
record prose and `.rel-group-hint`, neither of which this piece touches — same count as the
round-1 critic measured.

Two gate scripts also lost their footing when D4 backfilled every live record and no record
was left without a human area: `check-keys` silently dropped four empty-state checks (it
looked at bugs only), and `screenshot.mjs` turned "the key is skipped" into "unknown screen
— FAILED" one line later for anyone passing `--only human-empty`. Both fixed: the empty
state is looked for across all three kinds and the miss is printed when there is none, and
`--only` is validated against a catalogue of every key the script knows rather than against
the keys this project happens to be able to fill.

Measured on a five-item fixture (one retold, four legacy — built through the CLI into a
sandboxed `AGENTMON_REGISTRY_DIR`): the human half is now 701px against the agent half's
760px, with one notice of 203px and four one-line rows, where before it was 2.5× the agent
half.

## For humans

The board where complaints about this app itself are filed opened on its plain-language side. Every item nobody had retold yet printed the same explanation of what was missing. Its own headline, its own paragraph, its own command, its own two buttons. On a test board of five items, one retold and four not, that stacked four near-identical boxes. The list grew from 640 pixels tall to 1,631, and the instruction outweighed the five titles it hung off by about five to one.

**The board reads as a board again.** An item nobody has retold says so in four words on its own row, styled `.feedback-none`. The explanation is given once above them, in a panel called `BoardHumanNotice`, with one command instead of four. On that same test board the plain side is now shorter than the technical side: 701 pixels against 760.

It is like a shop notice pinned once by the door instead of taped to every shelf.

**It also stops guessing on your behalf.** The board opens on whichever side most of it is written in. Before, one item in five having a retelling switched everyone to plain language. A tie falls to the technical side, the one every item is guaranteed to have.

**And where a board holds some of each, the button says so before you press it.** It carries the number of rows that have been retold, the way the tabs beside it already carry theirs.

**A corrected record owns up on both sides.** The line saying a record has been corrected used to appear only on the technical side. So a reader who cannot read that side was shown a story the record itself says is wrong, with nothing admitting it. It is on both now, and following it carries you across to the correction.

**Two of our own checks had gone quiet.** Once every live record had been given a plain half, `check-keys` ran out of "nothing written yet" screens to look at, and stopped saying so. `screenshot.mjs` reported a screen it deliberately skips as a spelling mistake. Both now say plainly what they could not find.

`check-keys` drives the app by keyboard alone and passes 271 checks; seventeen are about this switch and three of those are new — the correction line on the technical side, on the retelling, and following it across. On a five-item board built for the purpose, all four of the recovered checks run again. `check-clipping` found nothing cut off at eight window widths in either language.

A designed empty screen, repeated four times, is not a designed screen.
