## What

Five fixes and two gates, all of them about Korean the app did not write.

* `word-break: keep-all` + `overflow-wrap: break-word` on `.prose`, `.project-desc`,
  `.now-row-title` and `.feed-summary` (src/styles/tokens.css). These four were excluded
  from the keep-all law on purpose; the exclusion is gone and its stated reason is rewritten.
* `flex: 0 1 auto` back on `.tip-value` and `.tip-label` (src/styles/app.css). Round 6 set
  them to `flex: none` and made English tooltips paint outside their card.
* Counters on `dash.countPart`, `dash.agentBarTip` and (through countPart) `dash.dayMix`.
* `HourBars` prints the busiest hour, not the drawing floor: `peak` and `scale` are two
  numbers now.
* One `agentInitials()` for the avatar's monogram, and a Hangul handle wears one syllable.

And the two gates that let all of it through:

* check-i18n asks its word-integrity question of the whole page rather than of the app's own
  words, and runs the whole width sweep a second time against a vault whose records are
  Korean — built for the run with the release CLI (scripts/ko-vault.mjs, new).
* check-clipping opens each chart tooltip at seven crosshairs, both ends included, and fails
  on ink painted outside the card. Both languages, so `npm run check:clipping` now runs ko
  then en the way check:i18n does.

## Why

The Korean typography law stopped at the app's own words. tokens.css said the record's text
"belongs to the agent who wrote it, this app does not reflow it beyond what the browser does
for any paragraph" — but the browser breaks English only at spaces and Korean between any two
syllables, so "the same as any paragraph" is worse-than-English, not neutral. Measured: a
vault of Korean records gave 144 mid-syllable splits over 13 screens x 7 widths, and this
repo's own English vault gave 35 wherever it quotes Korean; the chrome beside them, which
carries keep-all, gave zero at the same widths.

Underneath that: nothing here had ever pointed the app at a vault holding Korean records, so
six rounds of Korean review read Korean chrome over English data. That is a missing fixture,
not a missing assertion, which is why the fix is a vault the gate builds rather than one more
selector on a list.

The tooltip regression is the other half of the same lesson: `flex: none` was added last
round to stop Korean breaking inside the tip, `keep-all` was already doing that job, and the
only thing `flex: none` bought was an English label that could no longer wrap at its own
spaces — 32 readings of "done or abandoned" painted over the plot at 1280 and up.

## How

Every fix was reproduced before it was written and measured after, at the widths the critic
named, with two dev servers: the live vault read-only, and the Korean fixture.

The line-break question is asked by geometry, not by CSS: a Range is laid over each run of
two or more Hangul syllables and asked for its client rects, and rects that do not overlap
vertically are two lines. Line membership moved from an equal `top` to vertical overlap,
because a number set larger than the word beside it shares its line without sharing its top.

The Korean fixture is a payments team and a delivery team writing up their own work through
the CLI — Korean project names and descriptions, titles, `## What/## Why/## How`, progress
notes, outcomes, bug reports, resolutions and handles. It is built in a temp directory and
deleted when the gate ends; `./vault` is never written to.

## For humans

A record written in Korean came apart on screen in the middle of its words. A browser may break a Korean line between any two syllables, unless the page forbids it with `word-break: keep-all`. English only ever breaks at a space. The app used that rule over its own labels and buttons, and left what an author wrote alone on purpose. Leaving Korean alone is not neutral.

It is like a typesetter free to split any word at any letter, with no hyphen to warn you.

**A project whose records are written in Korean gave 144 broken words**, over 13 screens at seven widths. This project's own English records gave 35, wherever they quote Korean. Four surfaces took the rule — a record's body, a project's description and two summary lines — and the count went to zero.

**Nothing here had ever pointed this app at Korean records.** Six rounds of Korean review had read Korean furniture over English content. What was missing was not another rule but a stand-in. `scripts/ko-vault.mjs` builds a pretend project for each run, a payments team and a delivery team writing up their work in Korean, in a temporary folder that is deleted afterwards. The live records are never touched.

**The stand-in had a blind spot of its own.** Its records pointed at nothing, so the rail that lists related records stood empty on every screen the check opened, and an empty box passes a question about words. The first pass had missed exactly that box: `.rel-title`, a record's title drawn small a second time in that rail, four lines deep. Those records point at each other now, and a surface that comes up empty is reported as a gap.

**`npm run check:i18n` asks the page, not the stylesheet.** It lays a marker over each run of two or more Korean syllables and asks where it landed; back on two lines means the word was split, whatever the rules allowed. Ninety-seven widths across four record screens: 45 splits before that title was fixed, none after.

**A later round caught the same fault on the dated line that heads a finished record's outcome** — the line reading 등록 5시간 48분 후. It was breaking its own words across a narrow band of widths the sweep stepped straight over. That check had always opened the first bug on the board, and the board puts unresolved ones first, so no finished bug's line had ever been drawn in front of it. It picks a resolved one on purpose now, and two widths inside that band joined the sweep. Measured in two-pixel steps across it: 74 splits before, none in 1558 readings after.

**Korean counts things with a counter word, and the dashboard printed bare numbers.** The last-day line now reads 시작 15건 · 완료 16건 · 노트 30건, and ends 에이전트 6명 — events take 건, people take 명. A day heading and each agent's bar count the same way. English is byte for byte what it was.

**The busiest-hour line printed the wrong number entirely.** What it showed was not the count but the height the bars are drawn against, and that height reads one where nothing happened at all — so a project with an empty day announced one event. Those are two numbers now: an idle project says 가장 바쁜 시간대 이벤트 0건, and a busy one still says 9건 over its nine-event hour. Print the drawing height again and the phantom event comes straight back.

**Two Korean agents wore the same badge.** The small round mark beside a name is built from the parts of the handle, and a Korean part gave nothing, so `nova-배송팀` and `nova-결제팀` both came out a lone `n`. One rule does every badge now, on the row and in the quick-search alike: those two read `N배` and `N결`, a wholly Korean handle wears one syllable, and `p0-foundation-builder` still wears `PF`.

**A Korean project name met a button that would not press.** A project's name is also turned into its folder name, and that may hold only lowercase letters, digits, `-` and `_`; a Korean name leaves nothing behind. The form used to sit there with a dead button and no reason given. It now says it cannot make one from this name and asks you to type it — and on a throwaway copy, 결제 화면 재작성 with `gyeolje-hwamyeon` typed in creates the project.

**The chart's hover box needed two undoings.** A change from the round before stopped it shrinking, and English labels painted outside their card in 32 of 504 readings. Then near a chart's edge the card sliced 19 pixels off the box itself: it was placed a fixed distance in from the end, which assumes a wider chart than two side-by-side ones leave. It is now held inside the room there is. `npm run check:clipping` opens every one of those boxes in both languages, and asks a second question the ink test could not — whether the box sits inside everything that clips it.

A rule you apply only to your own words is a rule you have not finished writing.
