## What

`agentmon doctor`'s human-area length warning counts **one telling** now, not a whole record.
`crates/agentmon-core/src/human.rs` gains `longest_telling()` — the longest run of words
between bold lead-ins, with `body::Fences` tracked so a `**` inside a code block starts
nothing — and `WORDS_MAX` (450) is documented as the ceiling on a telling, never on a
record's total. `crates/agentmon-core/src/doctor.rs` reads that count into
`HumanGaps.long_telling`, and its fix line teaches the contract's real order: split before you
cut, and never cut a fact to reach a number.

docs/HUMAN_STYLE.md settles the definition the code needed. A record is compound wherever it
names the several things it shipped — its title, its What, or a list further down, an
"Alongside:" under How — not only in its title, which called WORK-0067 single-story and failed
it at 513 words. The compact block folds the ceiling clause into the budget line, so it is 20
lines between the markers again rather than 21. docs/AGENT_MANUAL.md's own 450 sentence says
the same thing, and the note human-area-enforcement is rewritten in place, both halves.

Four records repaired against round-10 findings, each through the CLI as `human_updated`:
WORK-0007 (the resolution banner does not end the bug page — `<RelatedSection>` renders after
`<ResolutionCard>` in src/pages/BugDetailPage.tsx), handoff-v1-release (the deleted clause
saying why the exists-probe mattered is back), BUG-0004 (the second copy of the phrase is
placed where the record places it, without naming a record the record does not name),
WORK-0055 (`AgentCodeGUI` named as the Why names it, instead of "another of their apps").

## Why

One binary taught two contradicting rules. `agentmon human-style` printed "A ceiling bounds
one telling, never a record's total", and the same build reported "2 record(s) have a human
area longer than the style contract's 450 words: WORK-0007 (824 words), WORK-0067 (513
words)" with the hint "cutting first a name carrying no fact of its own". So
`agentmon doctor --strict` exited 1 on this project, and it failed exactly the two records the
round had just repaired.

That hint is measured, twice, as a net loss: cutting toward the ceiling bought no ease
(easier 20/27 → 23/27 for the plain-English rewrite) and cost coverage (25/27 → 21/27, ours
outright overall 6 → 4). It is also the mechanism that dropped two of WORK-0007's five
deliverables whole in round 9, and no gate would have caught the re-drop. A gate whose false
positive is "cut a fact you owed" is worse than no gate.

## How

- `human::longest_telling()` walks the human area line by line. A bold lead-in at the top of a
  block ends the run before it; a `-` or `+` in front of one is furniture, a `*` is not,
  because it is the lead-in's own first character. `**` anywhere else in a block is emphasis
  inside a beat.
- It is a *floor* on the longest telling, and that is the safe direction. A run never spans two
  tellings, because a telling that follows another opens with a lead-in of its own — so past
  the ceiling some telling certainly is over, while under it the record may be long only
  because it covered everything it shipped. The one false positive this check could have is
  the failure the contract was rewritten to stop.
- The wall it was built for is still caught whole: prose with no beats in it is one run, so the
  703-word block that put a number in this file at all counts as 703.
- `write_roundtrip.rs`: the ceiling test now pins the compound case too — four beat-blocks,
  three times the ceiling in total, zero warnings — and asserts the fix line says "never a
  record's total", "beat-block" and "Never cut a fact to reach a number". `human.rs` gains a
  unit test for lead-ins, walls, fences, bullets and mid-block bold.

## For humans

The program that checks these records told writers to do the opposite of the writing guide it hands them. It read the plain-language half of a record, counted the words on the whole page, and complained about anything past 450.

**One page here really does hold five separate pieces of work.** `WORK-0007`, the record of a day spent rebuilding the bug screens, describes five of them. When the complaint arrived at 824 words, the agent sent to answer it cut two of the five out whole, and the facts underneath them went too.

**Nothing on a page says where one piece of work stops and the next starts.** A writer opens each part with a short bold sentence, and the same sentence opens the next stage of one story and a whole new piece of work. So the count now measures the longest stretch between those bold openings, and complains only when one stretch passes 450. A page that is long because it covered everything says nothing to it.

It is like judging a book by its longest chapter instead of by how many pages it has.

**A wall of words is still caught.** Writing with none of those bold openings counts as one stretch, and that is the shape this limit was put here for: a 703-word block nobody had broken up, which only a person counting by hand had noticed.

**The advice printed beside the complaint was the dangerous half.** It used to say cut a name first, then a fact. It now says split the page before cutting anything, and never cut a fact to reach a number.

**Four places were saying different things, and only one of them was the counter.** The writing guide now settles when a page counts as several pieces of work: whenever the page names them, in its title, in its summary, or in a list further down. Reading only titles had called one such record a single story and complained about it at 513 words. The handbook agents are given, and the shared note describing this guard, were both rewritten to match.

**Four records were repaired in the same pass, each against a reviewer's finding.** That same rebuilt-screens record no longer says a bug's fix report ends the page: the list of related records is drawn after it. The shared note on how to cut a release got back a sentence an earlier trim had thrown out, the one saying why one of its steps matters. `BUG-0004` now puts a repeated phrase where the record itself puts it, and stops naming a record that record never names. And `WORK-0055` describes the other program it takes its look from the way its own reasons describe it, instead of calling it "another of their apps".

**`cargo test --workspace` is the check that would have caught this.** That command runs every automatic check the program has, and one of them is new: it writes a page of four separate parts, three times the old limit long, and fails if the checker says a word about it. All 168 pass. The tools other agents call pass all 201 of theirs, and a separate check confirms that the guide the program prints and the document on disk are the same bytes.

`agentmon doctor --strict`, which reads a whole project and refuses it if anything is wrong, went from refusing this one to reporting all 109 records clean.

What no program can tell is whether a long page covered a lot or padded. Somebody still has to read it.

A rule that punishes covering everything teaches you to leave things out.
