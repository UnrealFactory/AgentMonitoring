## What

Stop printing UTC on screen. src/lib/format.ts renders formatDateTime/formatDate from
local date parts with a 12-hour clock (오전/오후 in Korean, am/pm in English); the UTC
string survives only in formatDateTimeUtc, which every tooltip already uses. In
src/lib/dashboard.ts the chart formatters (HOUR_FMT, DAY_FMT, DAY_FULL_FMT, DATE_FMT),
floorTo, tick alignment and the feed's day grouping all move to the reader's wall time,
and the i18n strings that claimed "UTC" (dash.scope, dash.activityNote, chart.busiestHour,
chart.hourTip, chart.summary) drop the claim.

## Why

A note updated at 2026-08-21T09:32:47Z showed "09:32" in the note detail sidebar while
Explorer said 오후 6:32 for the same file — the user read it as the app being three hours
wrong, nine hours in truth. UTC-on-screen also filed an evening's work under the next
day's date in the feed, since KST midnight is 15:00Z.

## How

Storage stays UTC ISO8601 per SPEC; only rendering converts. floorTo shifts into wall
time before flooring so day buckets break at local midnight and 6h buckets start on local
00/06/12/18; axisTicks applies the same shift, otherwise no day bucket would ever satisfy
the epoch modulo in a +9 timezone. Verified with tsc, check:keys (the one failure
pre-exists on a clean tree), check:clipping clean in both locales, and screenshots.

## Outcome

All screens now show the reader's local time as 오전/오후 h:mm (en: h:mm am/pm); tooltips keep the exact UTC stamp; day groups and chart buckets/ticks cut at local midnight. Verified: tsc clean, check:keys 255/256 (failure pre-exists), check:clipping clean ko+en, screenshots show 오전 11:10 note meta and 오전 2시/오전 8시/오후 2시 hour ticks.

## For humans

A note's details panel said the note was last touched at 09:32. Windows' own file browser, looking at the very same file, said 오후 6:32 — half past six in the evening. The person reading it took the app to be three hours out. It was nine.

**Every time the app printed was UTC, the one shared clock all the stamps are stored in.** The same fault put an evening's work under the next day's date in the activity feed. Midnight in Seoul falls at three in the afternoon on that clock.

**Screens now show your own clock, on a twelve-hour dial.** `src/lib/format.ts` is the one place that turns a stored stamp into text on a screen. It now builds that text from local date parts — 오전 or 오후 in Korean, am or pm in English. The labels that used to promise UTC dropped the word. What is stored did not change, as `SPEC` requires; only what is drawn converts.

It is like a diary kept in one agreed time for everyone, then read out to each person in their own.

**The charts were the awkward part.** Their bars are made by chopping time into equal blocks counted from a fixed instant long ago. A day-length block only lands on a real day if the chopping happens in the reader's clock. Nine hours ahead, no day block would ever have lined up. The little marks along the bottom edge needed the same shift, or the labels and the bars would have disagreed.

**I read the result off the screens.** The screenshots show a note stamped 오전 11:10. The hour marks read 오전 2시, 오전 8시, 오후 2시 — two and eight in the morning, two in the afternoon. `tsc`, which refuses code that no longer fits together, passes. `check:clipping`, which hunts for text spilling out of its box, came back clean in both languages.

**Times on every screen are now the reader's own, and hovering still gives the exact stored stamp.** `check:keys` ends at 255 of 256. That single failure was already there on an untouched copy of the code, so it is not this work.

Store one clock for everybody; show each person theirs.
