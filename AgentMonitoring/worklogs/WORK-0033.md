## What

The 프로젝트 삭제 dialog shipped last round could be navigated out from under, opened over,
and dismissed by the wrong Escape. This round makes it own the window, and adds the modal
distinction that makes that statable:

* `src/lib/modal.ts` grows a **kind** — `menu` or `dialog` — beside the depth count, with
  `isDialogOpen()` / `dialogDepth()`. A menu is the shallowest surface in the app (its layer
  is z-index 70, a dialog's scrim is 80); a dialog owns the window.
* `src/App.tsx`: the app's Back gesture (Alt+Left) asks `isModalOpen()` first and swallows
  the key while a modal is up, rather than navigating the page behind the scrim.
* `src/components/CommandPalette.tsx`: Ctrl+K (and the sidebar's search button) will not open
  the palette over a dialog. It still opens over a menu, which stands down for it (P8 round 2).
* `src/components/ContextMenu.tsx`: the menu takes its lock as a `menu`, and `open()` refuses
  while a dialog is up — a menu raised under a dialog's scrim is invisible and still holds
  the keyboard.
* `src/components/DeleteProject.tsx`: the dialog reads its own place in the stack; anything
  above it answers the keyboard first, a dialog arriving over it makes it stand down, a route
  change under it closes it, and typing with the focus dropped on `<body>` lands in the
  confirm box. Its `onClose` is now stable so the window listeners are not re-registered on
  every keystroke.
* `scripts/check-keys.mjs`: a new section, "the dialog owns the window", and the positive
  case beside it (with nothing open, Alt+Left still goes back).
* `SPEC.md`: screen 7's delete clause says the dialog owns the window.

## Why

The round-2 critic reproduced, on disk, the worst thing this app can do. With the delete
dialog open and armed on /projects for `altleft-victim`, one Alt+Left — the desktop app's
only Back gesture, added deliberately in App.tsx and bound to `window` with no modal check —
moved the page behind the scrim to another project's screen and left the armed dialog on top
of it. ↵ from there deleted the project the dialog was about, from a screen about Relay. I
reproduced it before touching anything: `/projects` → Alt+Left → `/p/relay`, dialog still up,
still armed, `altleft-victim on disk: false` after one ↵.

Two more of the same shape were live:

* Ctrl+K opened the palette over the dialog and left both on screen; the first Escape closed
  the *dialog underneath* rather than the palette on top — the exact wrong-layer-answers-
  Escape defect this repo fixed for context menus in P8 round 2.
* Selecting the `projects/<slug>` the dialog prints in its own warning and right-clicking it
  raised the copy menu *below the scrim*: nothing visible changed, the keyboard left the
  dialog for a menu nobody could see (`focus: button.ctx-item`), and the next Escape closed
  the dialog and left the menu. Found while fixing the first two.

All three are one contract, written in modal.ts and ignored by three call sites: anything
that listens on `window` asks first. A modal that can be navigated out from under is not a
modal, whatever it does with Escape.

## How

The choice worth recording is **why the dialog does not simply stand down** the way the
context menu does. Closing on any deeper modal would mean a copy menu raised on the dialog's
own text dismisses the confirmation the reader is halfway through typing into. So the stack
learned the difference between a menu and a dialog: menus may be opened over (and close
themselves), dialogs may not be opened over at all. That is one boolean and two accessors,
and it turns three special cases into one rule.

The rest follows from it. Ctrl+K over a dialog does nothing — which is what a modal dialog
means, and it keeps the P8 behaviour where Ctrl+K over a *menu* opens the palette. The
context menu's `open()` is the single choke point for every surface's right-click, so one
check covers the row menus, the selection menu and Shift+F10 together. The dialog keeps a
`modalDepth() > mine` guard on its own Escape/Tab handler anyway, so that if something is
ever allowed over it, the layer on top answers first rather than the layer underneath.

Belt and braces on the reported defect: the dialog also closes if the route beneath it
changes at all. Nothing can change it now, but a confirmation that outlives the screen it
was raised from is precisely how "delete this project" comes to be answered on a screen
about another one.

## For humans

`src/components/DeleteProject.tsx`, the delete confirmation, sat open on the projects screen. `altleft-victim` — a throwaway project on a copy of the records — was typed into its box and its button was armed. One press of `Alt+Left`, the app's only Back gesture, moved the page underneath to a different project's screen. The box stayed on top, still armed, and one press of Enter from there deleted that project from a screen about something else. A reviewing agent found it; I reproduced it before touching anything.

**The Back gesture listened to the whole window and never asked what was in front of it.** `Ctrl+K`, the shortcut for the app's search box, did the same. It opened that box over the confirmation, and the first Escape then closed the confirmation underneath rather than the box on top.

**Now `src/lib/modal.ts` knows two kinds of thing can be open.** A `menu` is the shallow kind: something may open over it, and it steps aside. A `dialog` — a window asking you to confirm something — is the other kind, and nothing may open over it at all. Back now swallows the key while one is up, `Ctrl+K` opens nothing over it, and a right-click raises no menu beneath it. `SPEC.md`, the written description this app's screens are built to, now says the same in the delete clause of its screen 7.

It is like a queue at a counter, where the person at the front answers and not someone further back.

**The obvious fix would have thrown your typing away.** Making the confirmation step aside for anything deeper sounds right. Then you select the folder path printed in its own warning and right-click to copy it. That opens a copy menu — and the confirmation you were halfway through typing into would have vanished. One rule replaced three special cases.

**That copy menu turned out to be a third fault.** It was opening beneath the dimmed backdrop, where nobody could see it, while quietly holding the keyboard. The next Escape then closed the confirmation and left the menu.

**Every case was measured before and after, on a copy of the records.** Before, the page moved and the project was off the disk after one Enter. After, the page stays and the button stays armed. `scripts/check-keys.mjs`, the run that drives the app by keyboard and reads what lands on screen, gained fifteen checks in each language. It also gained the opposite case: with nothing open, Back still goes back.

**I ran the whole thing once in the real desktop window.** On a copy of the records with a throwaway project, `desk-victim`, I opened the row's own menu, typed into the box to arm the button, then pressed Back and `Ctrl+K` and watched the confirmation sit there untouched. Then I deleted for real: the row left the projects screen and the left-hand column, a message said it was deleted, and the folder was gone from the disk. The desktop side printed its own line too, naming the project and the one work log, no bugs and two events that went with it. One last Back, with nothing open, landed on the gone project's address and drew the screen designed for a project that is not there.

**What is different: the confirmation holds the window until you answer it.** It also closes itself if the screen beneath it ever changes, because a question that outlives its screen is how this went wrong. Nothing was deleted outside temporary copies.

A question you can walk away from is not a question the app really asked.
