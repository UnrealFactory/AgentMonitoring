## What

The human half now has a reading scale of its own that steps with the window, and the sheet
that carries it fills the record column instead of stopping at its own measure.

- `src/styles/tokens.css` — `--text-read` is documented and set as *one step above whatever
  `.prose` is at this width*, and two new tokens carry the ramp inside the sheet:
  `--text-read-lead` (beat headings, closing line) and `--text-read-lede` (the opening
  paragraph). All three, plus `--human-measure`, are re-declared on `:root` inside the two
  wide-window queries in `src/styles/app.css`, the way `--content-max` already is:
  15/18/20 + 62ch below 1560, 16/19/21 + 66ch at 1560, 17/20/22 + 70ch at 1840.
- `.human-sheet` and `.human-empty` lost their `max-width` and gained
  `padding-inline: max(--space-8, (100% - measure) / 2)`, so each card is exactly the column
  wide and its content box is exactly one measure, centred.
- `src/lib/sections.ts` grew a `sentence` option (`splitLabelledSections(src, { min: 1,
  sentence: true })` from `src/lib/human.ts`): a lead-in may run to 140 characters instead of
  a label's 80, and its bound clause may close on the author's own full stop as well as on a
  colon. Resolutions pass neither flag and split byte-identically.
- `src/lib/tablist.ts` (new) — one `onKeyDown` for all six `.segmented` `role="tablist"`
  controls: ← → move and press, Home/End jump, ↑ ↓ deliberately unbound. Wired into
  `HumanView.tsx` and the five list/board/dashboard toolbars.
- `rec.referencedByHint` and `view.peekNote` mark the interpolated record id as an id
  (backticks + `RichText`, so it lands in the mono face); `scripts/check-i18n.mjs`'s CHROME
  rule now means "this element's *attributes* are the app talking", with the element's own
  text subtracted from them.
- `.feedback-human` damps the page ramp to the reading size and drops the per-row byline.

## Why

D3's round-3 critic measured both halves in a live browser and found the step the reading
token exists for was zero: at 1600px (the width every reference shot is taken at) the agent
prose and the retelling were both 15px/24px, and at 1900px the agent half was **16px** while
the retelling stayed 15 — the half that is read set smaller than the half that is scanned,
with the sheet's own headings (`--text-lg`, fixed) level with the agent half's paragraphs.
Cause: `.human-view .prose { font-size: var(--text-read) }` (0,0,2,0) silently outranks this
app's own wide contract on `.prose` (0,0,1,0), and `--text-read` was a constant.

The same accident had a second symptom: a fixed `--human-measure` inside a column that grows
left a dead strip of app floor down one side of the sheet — 71px at 1400, 237px at 1600,
405px at 1900 — the only screen in the app whose empty space widens with the window, which is
the defect WORK-0041 was written to remove.

Three secondary gaps from the same review: check-i18n exited 1 (39 findings, one of them on a
screen this piece added); `role="tablist"` promised arrows that none of the app's six
segmented controls delivered; and 41 of the 583 bold lead-ins in this repo's own retellings
were silently left as inline body text beside siblings drawn as numbered headings.

## How

Measured first, in a real browser, at 960/1152/1400/1600/1900/2560 on a fixture project and
on this repo's own records, and reproduced the critic's numbers exactly before changing
anything (`.detail-main` width, `.prose` box, `.human-sheet` box, computed sizes both halves).

The reading scale is declared relative to the body it sits beside, so it cannot drift from it
again; the sheet is centred by percentage padding rather than by a wrapper element, so the
content box lands exactly one measure wide with no extra DOM. `.human-empty` got the same
treatment plus one right edge for all four of its lines — its caps were `--human-measure`
in `ch`, which resolved against each element's own size and came out in three places.

The i18n gate's CHROME entry was written for a stale chip's **tooltip** and was reading the
chip's text (the author's id) as well; the fix was verified by breaking `rec.missingRefTip`'s
Korean on purpose and confirming the gate still fails on it (32 findings), then reverting.

Beat promotion re-measured over all 106 live retellings: 542/583 before, 583/583 after, and
`scripts/markdown-smoke.mjs` holds at the same 722 checks with the same single pre-existing
WORK-0061 failure.

## For humans

A record here has two halves: the technical one, and a retelling in plain words for someone who was not there. The plain half is the one you sit and read, and on a wide screen it was set in smaller letters than the technical half. The panel holding it left a strip of bare background that widened with the window.

**The reading size was written down once, as a number.** This app grows its own text with the window: 14, then 15, then 16. The plain half was meant to sit one step above that, but its size was a fixed 15, and the rule setting it outranked the rule that grows. So at the width every screenshot here is taken at, both halves were 15; one step wider, the technical half was 16 and the plain half still 15.

**The size is now a comparison, not a number.** It is one step above whatever the record body is at that width and moves when the body moves: 15, 16, 17. The headings inside the plain half move with it, 18, 19, 20.

**The panel is now as wide as the column it sits in.** It used to stop at the width of its own text, leaving bare background beside it: 71 pixels on a small window, 405 on a large one. Now the panel fills the column and the text sits centred inside it.

It is like moving a photograph from the edge of a noticeboard onto a page with margins: the same paper, nothing missing.

**The feedback board is deliberately left out of that growth.** Its rows stay at the reading size rather than stepping up with the window, and a row no longer repeats the name of whoever retold that item.

**Sentences the writers had bolded were being dropped as headings.** Each part of a retelling opens with one bold sentence, which the app promotes to a numbered heading. The rule came from labelling a bug's fix report, where a label is a word or two, so it threw away any opening over 80 characters. That quietly lost 41 of the 583 openings here. An opening may now run a sentence's length and end where its author ended it; all 583 are headings.

**A check on language was red, and one of its complaints was about a screen this round had just added.** `check-i18n` reads every screen in each language and flags the words left in the wrong one; it stood at 39 complaints. Two of the app's own sentences print a record's id inside them, and that id was being counted as an English word — both now mark it as an id, which also sets it in the app's typewriter face. The rule underneath was reading a hover label together with the chip it hangs off; it now takes only what the app itself says, with the element's own words subtracted. Both languages come back clean.

**I checked that the gate had not simply gone blind.** I put one of those Korean hover labels back into English on purpose. The check failed on it, 32 complaints, and I put the Korean back.

I checked the sizes by opening the app in a real browser at six widths, on a made-up project and on this project's own history. The arrow keys now move along the little rows of buttons too, all six of them, from one shared piece of code; `check-keys`, which drives this app by keyboard alone, passes 276 checks, five more than before.

Still uneven: the number beside each part sits inside the text column rather than out in the margin, so the opening paragraph starts a little left of the parts under it. One check of the drawn text still fails on one record, as it did before this work.

A size written as a number stops being a comparison the moment the thing it was compared to moves.
