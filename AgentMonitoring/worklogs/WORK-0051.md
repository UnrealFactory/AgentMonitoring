## What

Flip the timeline rails on the work and bug detail pages. Work: the end edge (done /
still running · so-far duration) is now the top of the trail, notes run newest to
oldest with their chronological numbers kept, and the started edge closes the bottom;
the Outcome card moves above the trail. Bug: the ResolutionCard moves above the thread,
the thread runs end-marker/newest comment down to the filed edge, and the handover
sentence becomes "the fix is recorded above" (bd.fixBelow → bd.fixAbove). Both pages'
contents rails reordered to match.

## Why

The owner's call, and the right one for a monitoring app: a reader opening a running
record (ElmwoodOnline's WORK-0007, screen-length notes per round) is there for the
latest round, and reaching it meant scrolling past everything already read. Every list
and feed in the app is already newest-first. Flipping only the notes would have broken
the start→finish frame, so the whole rail flips: one rule, further down is further back.

## How

Pure JSX/order changes — the rail CSS trims its line at first/last child generically,
so no styles moved. The bug thread's tie rule inverts with the sort (end marker wins a
tie, drawing above the comment written before it), and the "fix above" handover now
keys on the end node being first rather than last. Verified: tsc, check:keys 257,
check:clipping clean ko+en, canonical shots re-taken and read.

## Outcome

Work and bug detail trails now read newest-first with the current state as the top edge and the origin at the bottom; Outcome/Resolution cards sit above their trails and the TOCs match. Verified: tsc clean, check:keys 257/257, check:clipping clean in both locales, work/bug full-page shots re-taken and visually confirmed (note numbering stays chronological).

## For humans

Open a record still being worked on and you are there for the newest note. On `WORK-0007`, a work log in another project, ElmwoodOnline, the newest note sat at the bottom. Every round of work there leaves a note about a screen long. Reaching the newest meant scrolling past everything you had already read. The owner made the call: turn the page around.

**A work log now opens on where it stands today.** The top of the trail says finished, or still running and for how long so far. Under that come the notes, newest first, and the moment the work started closes the page at the bottom. A bug page does the same: `ResolutionCard`, the block holding the fix, sits above the conversation. The conversation runs from the newest message down to the day it was filed.

Every other list in the app already ran newest first. It is like a stack of paper on a desk: the last thing you put down is the first thing you pick up.

**Turning only the notes around would have broken the frame.** The start of the work and the end of it are drawn as the two ends of the trail. Leave those where they were and the page reads start, newest, oldest, end — backwards whichever way you go. So the whole thing flipped together, on one rule: further down the page is further back in time.

**Two smaller things turned with it.** The line at the foot of a bug's conversation used to send you down to the fix. It now points up, and is stored under the label `bd.fixAbove`. Where an end marker and a message carry the very same moment, the marker draws above rather than below. The list of contents on both pages was reordered to match.

**I re-took the screenshots and read them.** Full-page shots of a work log and of a bug, looked at with my own eyes. `check:clipping` hunts for text running out of the space drawn for it, so nobody meets a label with its last word sliced off. It came back clean in Korean and in English.

**Expect the numbers to count downwards.** Each note keeps the number it was given when it was written, so the newest note carries the highest one and sits at the top.

On a page about work still in progress, the top of the page should be today.
