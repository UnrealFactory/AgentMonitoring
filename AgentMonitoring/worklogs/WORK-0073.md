## What

Give a beat-less retelling the two things every other retelling on the human view has: a
closing line in its own accent block, and an opening paragraph that is bigger than the
paragraphs under it.

Two edits.

`src/lib/human.ts` — `readHumanStory` returned `takeaway: null` before `carveTakeaway` ever
ran when `beats.length === 0`. It now carves the closing line off `split.preamble` in that
case, and returns the remainder as the lede.

`src/styles/app.css` — `.human-view .prose.human-lede` sat on the whole opening *run*, so
every paragraph in it took `--text-read-lede`. The rule moved to
`.human-view .prose.human-lede > p:first-child`; the rest of the run inherits `--text-read`
and `--leading-normal` from `.human-view .prose`. `.feedback-human`'s override followed the
selector in, because a container rule cannot outrank a descendant's own `font-size`.

Three smaller things found while verifying:

- `scripts/screenshot.mjs` — `--record` now steers the `-human` keys as well as the detail
  keys. They took `richest()` (the longest retelling of each kind), which by construction can
  never reach a thin record, so no default run has ever photographed the page shape this
  round repaired. A named record with no retelling falls back to `richest()` and logs it.
- `scripts/check-i18n.mjs` — `.palette-meta-avatar` joins `.agent-avatar` on the
  author-content list. Both render `agentInitials()` and both are `aria-hidden`, but only one
  was listed, so the exemption depended on the letters an agent's handle starts with:
  `fable-notes-builder` gives "FN" and passed, `d3-round5` gives "DR", an English word, and
  failed the Korean run on a screen where nothing was wrong.
- `src/pages/NoteDetailPage.tsx` — a comment claimed the note lead and the sheet under it
  have one left edge. Their *cards* do; their text is 96px apart at 1600 and 138px at 1900.
  Comment corrected, layout unchanged.

## Why

`docs/HUMAN_STYLE.md` sanctions the beat-less shape in writing — "A thin record gets no
lead-ins at all" — one bullet above the one that asks every retelling to "close on a rule of
thumb, not a summary". The parser treated the two as one rule and withheld the closing line
from exactly the records the contract says will have no other structure.

Measured over the 107 live retellings with the shipped parser: 3 are beat-less (WORK-0060,
BUG-0023, chart-note-series-colour) and 3 of 3 end on a closing line inside the module's own
`MAX_TAKEAWAY` and prose test, at 77, 133 and 65 characters. All three were drawn as one more
grey paragraph.

The type half is the same defect in CSS. BUG-0023's whole retelling — 254 words, five
paragraphs — came out at `--text-read-lede`: 21px at 1600, 22px at 1900, against a 26px record
title and a 16px agent half, and above `--text-xl` (20px, a section-heading size) on a page
whose design system opens with "13-14px type". The reading sizes are allowed to exceed that
because they buy a ramp; a page set entirely at the top of the ramp has no ramp. 14 of the 107
open on more than one paragraph, so the fault was not confined to the beat-less three.

The project's own reference for the same source says what the page should be:
`progress/dual/baselines/note-chart-note-series-colour/eli5.png` renders it as named stages
plus the closing rule set large and bold.

## How

`carveTakeaway`'s existing floor is the whole safety: with no blank line in the source there
is no last *paragraph* to lift, so a one-paragraph retelling keeps its one paragraph, the
same way a one-paragraph beat keeps its body. Nothing new was needed to be careful.

Differential over all 107 live retellings, old parser against new, in one process: beats
571 -> 571 (unchanged), takeaways 99 -> 102, records whose parse changed 3 — the three
beat-less ones — and records whose rendered text changed 0, comparing the normalised
concatenation of lede + every beat + takeaway on both sides. Nothing is summarised, reordered
or dropped.

Rendered sizes read out of a real browser at 1152/1600/1900 over five records (the three
beat-less, one two-paragraph lede, one fully beated): opening paragraph 20/21/22px at
`--leading-snug`, every paragraph after it 15/16/17px at `--leading-normal`, beat headings
18/19/20px, closing line 18/19/20px on `--accent-soft`. First and last paragraph of the
opening run share both edges at every width, so the measure did not split. On the
app-feedback board the first opening paragraph reads 15/16/17 — the board's flattening still
lands on it through the new selector.

## For humans

Three of the plain-language write-ups here are short enough that the writing guide tells their author to use no bold headings at all. On screen those three came out as one more grey paragraph each: no larger sentence to open on, and the line each of them closes on drawn like any other line. A fourth fault sat beside it, on `BUG-0023`, the record of a bug in this app: all five of its paragraphs printed at the size meant for an opening sentence alone — 21 pixels, against a technical half at 16 and the record's own title at 26.

**The code read one rule where the guide has two.** The guide says a thin write-up gets no bold headings, and one line later asks every write-up to close on a sentence worth repeating. The part of the app that reads these pages treated those as a single rule, and withheld the closing line from exactly the write-ups that have no other shape to fall back on.

**The big opening size was sitting on the whole opening run.** A write-up may open on more than one paragraph — fourteen of the 107 here do — and every paragraph in that run was taking the opening size. It now lands on the first paragraph only, and the rest read at the ordinary reading size.

The three short write-ups now read like the long ones: one large sentence to open on, ordinary paragraphs under it, and the line each piece closes on set apart in its own tinted block.

**I checked it two ways, and neither of them was reading the stylesheet.** I ran the old code and the new code over all 107 of our write-ups at once and compared, character for character, what each one puts on the page: three changed shape, and none lost a word. Then I read the letter sizes off a real screen at three window widths, on a short write-up, a long one and one in between. They step the way the design asks.

**Every automatic check passed, on the real records and on a throwaway project.** `check-keys`, which drives the app by keyboard alone, scores 277 here and 279 there. `check-clipping` found nothing sliced off at eight window widths across 119 screens, and `check-i18n` came back clean in both languages over 225. The throwaway project exists for the one screen the real one can no longer show: the box that says a record has no plain half. None of our records is missing one any more, which is good news that made that box impossible to photograph.

**A screenshot run could never have photographed the shape this round repaired.** `screenshot.mjs` takes the app's reference pictures, and for each kind of record it picks the longest retelling it can find — which by construction is never a short one. Asking it for a particular record now steers the plain-half pictures as well as the technical ones. Ask for a record with no retelling and it falls back to the longest, and says in its output that it did.

**A language check was failing on the letters in an agent's name.** Two round badges draw an agent's initials, and both are hidden from anyone listening rather than looking, but only one of them was on the check's list of writing that belongs to the author. So the handle `d3-round5` gave "DR", which reads as an English word, and failed the Korean run on a screen where nothing was wrong, while `fable-notes-builder` gave "FN" and passed. The second badge is on that list now.

**A comment in the code claimed something the screen does not do.** It said a note's opening line and the sheet beneath it share one left edge. Their cards do; their text is 96 pixels apart at one window width and 138 at another. I corrected the comment and moved no layout.

**One check does fail, and it failed the same way before I touched anything.** A record whose own body quotes a piece of link punctuation as an example trips the checker that looks for lost text. I reverted my change and it failed identically. That one is about that record's words, not about this work.

A piece set at one size has no beginning and no end.
