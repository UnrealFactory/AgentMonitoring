## What

Close the last two gate failures and ship the release build:

1. The record renderer stops cutting an author's sentence in half. `splitLabelledSections`
   (`src/lib/sections.ts`) promoted a bold lead-in to a heading and dropped the clause that
   was glued to it into the body, so six live records rendered a heading "Fix" over a
   paragraph beginning ", in the new `src/lib/markdown-parse.ts`".
2. A Referenced-by title wraps instead of ellipsising, and an id written into prose carries
   the title of the record it names, so an append-only correction can be read and a wrong
   id is visible where it stands.
3. One word per state everywhere: a bug nobody holds is `unassigned` on all five surfaces
   that used to call it unclaimed, unassigned or "needs an owner", and a resolved bug is
   `resolved` in the byline, the side panel and the resolution banner, not `fixed`.
4. The command palette's meta column marks which value is a project and which is an agent.
5. The packaged window can set its own title again (`core:window:allow-set-title`), and the
   call that was denied for a whole round no longer swallows the rejection.

## Why

Three of these are the same defect wearing different clothes: the app printing something
other than what the vault says, on the screens whose whole job is to show a record
faithfully.

The section splitter is the worst of them. It is the one place the app loses to a merged
pull request on GitHub, which never splits an author's sentence in half — and the record it
disfigured worst is BUG-0004, *Record renderer rewrites the vault*, whose own resolution
promises that every other byte is rendered where the author put it. Six of 144 labelled
parts came back with a body opening on the author's comma; half of the twelve records that
write a clause after the label were broken.

The clipped Referenced-by title fails at the point of its own claim. This vault corrects a
wrong sentence by filing a record that points back at it, on the argument that "a reader who
arrives at the wrong sentence finds the right number one line below it". At every width the
app renders, that line read "WORK-0011 says relay is three weeks of ten agents; rela…" — the
page printing the wrong number twice and cutting off the right one. An append-only
correction that cannot be read is decorative.

The vocabulary misses are cheaper — a synonym costs a reader a half-second, a garbled
sentence costs the record its credibility — but they are the ones a reader meets first.

## How

`carveTrailer` in `src/lib/sections.ts` replaces the old `binds` test. A clause bound to the
label by `,;:` is carved off only at a boundary the author wrote: the end of the paragraph
(the clause rides on the heading, wrapped source lines and all) or a clause-closing colon
(the heading takes the clause up to the colon, the body starts at the next word). Colons
inside inline code and URLs do not close a clause. When neither boundary exists the label is
not promoted at all and the paragraph keeps its bold lead-in — a weaker landmark is a smaller
loss than a severed sentence. `scripts/markdown-smoke.mjs` gained four fixtures and a sweep
assertion that no split body begins with punctuation.

Inline ids get their titles from the index the record pages already load for the Related
block, handed down through a `RecordTitles` context, so no screen asks the vault for
anything new. An id no record answers to keeps its chip shape and loses its accent, so a
typo is visible in the sentence rather than only at the 404 it lands on.

`unassigned`, `Time to resolve` and the amber "unassigned for 6h" now come from
`src/lib/words.ts`, which is to the app's words what tokens.css is to its colours.

## For humans

Open a bug record and the page showed a heading, Fix, with a paragraph beneath it that began with a comma. The author had written a bold opening phrase and carried the sentence on from it. The app cut the sentence there and dropped the rest into the paragraph below. Six records read that way.

**The record it mangled worst was the one about this very promise.** `BUG-0004` is the bug titled "Record renderer rewrites the vault", and its own resolution promises that every other byte appears where the author put it.

**A clause is now cut only where the author put a break.** `carveTrailer`, in `src/lib/sections.ts`, is the part that splits a record into labelled parts. It lifts a bold phrase into a heading only at a break the author wrote. That means the end of the paragraph, or a colon closing the clause. Colons inside code and web addresses do not count, and where there is no such break nothing is lifted.

It is like taking a chapter title from a book's first line: you may cut at a full stop, never mid-breath.

**Reading the code was not enough; I had to read the finished page.** A heading is laid out as a row of parts, so a 122-character clause became a line of its own. The page still showed Fix above a line starting on a comma, the defect rebuilt in the styling. `.res-part-text` makes the label and its clause one run of text now.

**A correction nobody could read.** This history corrects a wrong sentence by filing a record that points back at it. On the page carrying the wrong sentence, `BUG-0017`'s pointer read "WORK-0011 says relay is three weeks of ten agents; rela…". It printed the wrong number twice and cut off the right one. `.rel-title` wraps those titles now instead of trimming them. An id written into a sentence also carries the title of the record it names, so you can see what is being pointed at without going there — and an id no record answers to keeps its shape but loses its colour, so a typo shows itself where it stands rather than only at the dead end it leads to.

**I counted rather than eyeballed.** Across both projects: 148 labelled parts, 0 bodies opening on punctuation where there were 6, 0 words lost. Then I read them back out of the running pages, all 56 of them: 151 parts drawn, none severed.

**A bug nobody owns had three names.** Unclaimed on one screen, unassigned on another, "needs an owner" on a third: five places, three words, one state. All five say unassigned now. A bug that has been dealt with is resolved everywhere, rather than fixed in some places and resolved in others, and a project's row says "last activity 3h ago" where it used to say "active 3h ago" directly under a heading that said Active. Those words live in one file, `src/lib/words.ts`, the way the colours live in one file.

**The search box's small print now says what each name is.** Under a result sat a line of grey values with nothing to mark which was a project and which was the worker who did the job. An agent wears the same ring of initials it wears everywhere else in the app now, a project keeps the folder mark from the left-hand column, and each part names its own kind when you hover it or when it is read aloud.
