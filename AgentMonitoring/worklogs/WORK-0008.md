## What

Fixing the two defects the P3 critics found — a bug board that cut agent names in half, and
a bug record whose verification evidence had no landmark — and making the record page's
closing section (Resolution on a bug, Outcome on a work log) navigable structure rather than
one wall of prose.

## Why

Both findings are the same failure in different clothes: the page looked right and told the
reader something the vault does not say.

The board's `.bug-row-people` was a 140px box with `overflow: hidden` wrapped around an
inline `.handoff`. An inline child is laid out at its natural width and never learns it is
being cropped, so `text-overflow: ellipsis` on `.agent-name` could not fire — the element
fits its own box, and the *ancestor* does the cutting. On this project's own board, three of
five rows read "p3-bugs-builc" and "p0-foundatior": a name sliced through a glyph, with
nothing to tell the reader anything was lost. Relay's agents are four letters, which is why
the previous round's screenshots never showed it.

The record page's failure was structural. A resolution is written as **Root cause.** …
**Fix**, … **Verified.** … — and rendered as inline bold inside one card, "Verified." has
exactly the weight of any other bold phrase. A reader reconstructing BUG-0004 recovered the
symptom, the root cause and all six fix steps, then reported *no verification evidence at
all*: the SIGKILL test (41 deliveries left in_flight, all 41 reclaimed 90s later, none lost,
none double-sent) is the proof of the fix's central trade, and it was invisible. The
contents rail made it worse by offering one flat "Resolution" row hardcoded to count 0 while
Thread and Related earned counts.

## How

The board: make the truncation happen where it is visible, and size the column from the
data. The people cell became a flex container with min-width 0 down to `.agent-name`, so the
ellipsis fires; its width now comes from the project's own agent names (src/lib/columns.ts),
because `nova` and `p0-foundation-builder` cannot share one number. The width estimate is
allowed to be wrong — it decides air, never correctness, since the ellipsis and the tooltip
are underneath it. The same latent defect was two pixels away in the work list's agent
column and got the same fix.

The record: src/lib/sections.ts recovers the structure the author already wrote. A paragraph
that opens with a short bold run becomes an anchored sub-section — heading, contents row and
count — and a clause bound to the label (", in `dispatcher.rs` and `queue.rs`:") stays on
the heading line. Fewer than two labels means the body is not written in sections and is
left exactly as it is. The verification part is drawn as evidence: a green panel, one check
per item, the count stated, figures in full-strength tabular numerals.

What this deliberately does NOT do is read numbers out of the prose to build a "94 → 3"
strip. Those deltas are written six different ways in one record ("peaked at 3, against 94
before"; "restored … of 8 to 32"; "back to 2,900 … from 940"), and a regex confident enough
to pair them is confident enough to pair the wrong ones. Printing a number the record does
not contain, on the screen whose whole job is fidelity, is the same class of defect as the
half-drawn name. The evidence gets a landmark instead; the figures stay in the sentences
that make them true.

## For humans

On this project's own list of bugs, three rows out of five showed an agent's name sliced through a letter: "p3-bugs-builc", "p0-foundatior". Nothing on the row said that anything had been cut.

**The "…" that marks a shortened name could not appear.** A name too wide for its column is normally trimmed and given those three dots. Here the cutting was done by `.bug-row-people`, the fixed-width box holding who filed a bug and who owns it. `.agent-name`, the name inside that box, was laid out at its full width and never learned it was too big.

It is like cropping a photograph with a sheet of paper: the photograph has no idea anything is missing.

**The cell trims its own name now**, so the dots appear where a reader can see them. `src/lib/columns.ts` sizes that column from the longest agent name the project actually uses. No screenshot had ever shown the fault, because the other project's agents are all four letters long.

**A reader read a fix report end to end and said it had never been tested.** On relay's `BUG-0004` they recovered the symptom, the cause and all six repair steps, then reported no evidence at all. It was there in bold, weighing exactly what every other bold phrase weighed. The program had been killed mid-flight. All 41 half-sent deliveries came back 90 seconds later, none lost and none sent twice.

**Bold labels are real sections now.** `src/lib/sections.ts` turns a paragraph that opens with a short bold label into a section. It gets its own heading and a count of what sits under it. A "Verified" label is drawn as a panel with one tick per item. A body with fewer than two such labels is left exactly as it was, because it was never written in sections.

**A bug's fix and a work log's summary are drawn by one piece of code**, so the two pages cannot drift apart. The list of sections beside the text used to point at the wrong one: it lit whichever section was highest on the screen, so while you read the evidence it still highlighted the heading above it. It now lights the last section whose top has crossed a line near the top of the window.

**What I refused to build was a strip of before-and-after figures.** Those figures are written six different ways inside one record. A pattern confident enough to pair them is confident enough to pair the wrong two.

**A count above a list disagreed with the list.** `BUG-0005`, which I filed against the work list a round earlier, was tabs counting every record in the project while the rows below them were narrowed by a filter: "Done 9" over a group of two. They count what a reader would actually get now. The manual an agent writes records from also states the bold-label rule outright, so the deal between the writer and the page is written down rather than remembered.

**Every screen is clean at five widths now.** `npm run check:clipping` opens each one at those five window sizes and measures where the ink lands. A "…" that never appears cannot hide from that. Before the fix it named both cut names and said by how much. It still reports one thing honestly: a table on the summary screen, filed as `BUG-0006` and unclaimed. And a narrowed bug board still cannot be shared — the address does not carry the filter, so a reload or a link hands you the whole board again.

A page can be laid out beautifully and still show you something that is not in the file.
