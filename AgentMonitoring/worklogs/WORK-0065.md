## What

SPEC.md gained the contract section; PLAN.md defines pieces D1-D6 with builder/critic loops.

## Why

The owner wants records readable by non-experts — our own eli5: it must beat the default eli5 plugin's output in blind A/B (omit less, read easier), hit jvns.ca retellability, and look native in the app. Enforced at save so no agent can skip it; taught at write time so no session pays for it.

## How

Pieces looped until they beat the bar (PLAN.md): D1 style contract judged by fresh-writer A/B vs cached eli5 baselines; D2 core/CLI/MCP enforcement judged by a rejection matrix in sandboxed fixtures; D3 toggle UI judged on rendered screens; D4 CLI-only backfill of all live records; D5 instruction-diet ablation; D6 end-to-end gate. All subagents opus/max; builders never self-grade.

## For humans

Every write-up in this project used to be written by AI agents for the next AI agent — dense notes full of file paths and shorthand. The owner asked for something more: every saved record must now also carry a plain-language version a person can just read, and the app gets a switch on each record page to flip between the two views.

We split the job into small pieces, and each piece gets two separate AIs: a builder, and a deliberately hard-to-please critic with fresh eyes who is the only one allowed to grade. A piece counts as finished when its output beats a popular "explain it simply" tool (eli5) on the same record — covering more of what happened while being easier to follow — and when a non-programmer who reads the result can retell the story correctly.

So far the plumbing is done: the tool agents use to save records now refuses any save that arrives without its plain-language part, whichever door it comes through, and the refusal message itself teaches the writing rules — so the rules cost nothing until the moment someone forgets them. The writing rulebook is in its test loop now, and the app's flip switch is being built next. This summary gets rewritten when the whole thing lands.
