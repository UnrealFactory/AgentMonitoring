## What

Owner directive 2026-08-22: every record (work, bug, note, app feedback) carries an agent area AND a human area; the app's record detail screens gain an Agent/Human toggle; any save path (CLI or MCP) rejects a record without its human area; the always-on instruction cost of this stays minimal (style contract is write-time reading). SPEC.md gained the contract section; PLAN.md defines pieces D1-D6 with builder/critic loops.

## Why

The owner wants records readable by non-experts — our own eli5: it must beat the default eli5 plugin's output in blind A/B (omit less, read easier), hit jvns.ca retellability, and look native in the app. Enforced at save so no agent can skip it; taught at write time so no session pays for it.

## How

Pieces looped until they beat the bar (PLAN.md): D1 style contract judged by fresh-writer A/B vs cached eli5 baselines; D2 core/CLI/MCP enforcement judged by a rejection matrix in sandboxed fixtures; D3 toggle UI judged on rendered screens; D4 CLI-only backfill of all live records; D5 instruction-diet ablation; D6 end-to-end gate. All subagents opus/max; builders never self-grade.

## For humans

On 22 August 2026 the owner settled how records are written here. Every record — work log, bug, note, complaint about the app itself — must carry a second half, written plainly for someone who does not program. Each record page is to get a switch between the two. No save may accept a record without it.

**The refusal is live on every way in.** The command line turns away a record with no plain half. So does MCP, the go-between other agents reach this app through. The writing rules are not session-start reading: the refusal prints them when needed.

The work was cut into six pieces, D1 to D6, each in `PLAN.md` with its own build-and-criticise loop. It is like six workshops on one street, each inspected by somebody who did not build it.

**D1, the writing rules, took sixteen rounds to pass.** The bar is a blind comparison against `eli5`, a ready-made tool that rewrites text to be simple. Three judges who wrote neither version mark both. Ours must cover more of what happened and read easier. Over the first six rounds coverage won all nine comparisons, and ease kept losing on the dense records.

**Round five overcorrected.** Reaching for ease, the writing dropped the names and numbers that let a reader check it. That cost coverage instead. The rules went back out with the judges' evidence attached.

**Two faults were in the judging, not the writing.** One record's plain half had been seeded early, and writers read it back as if the record said it. They now get copies with that half stripped out. The judges were also crediting invented detail as coverage; a rule against that was added.

**Round sixteen passed.** Fresh writers, handed only the style contract at `docs/HUMAN_STYLE.md`, beat the other tool on all three test records. Three judges out of three. Nothing they wrote was invented, and each retelling matched its record.

**D2, the refusing, passed earlier, at round five.** A table of saves that must be refused, and saves that must not, checked it on throwaway copies.

**What is left is the visible half.** D3, the switch on the record page, is in its first round of criticism. D4 is writing the plain halves for the roughly ninety-nine records already saved here, some held back for blind judging.

Still open: a record damaged by hand, its quoted block never closed, is refused by every save. But `doctor` — the command that reads every record here and reports what is broken — still ends by saying all is well, and only warns. It is D6's to clear.

A record only another agent can read is a record only another agent can check.
