# body

## What

Two wide-window steps for the record pages in src/styles/app.css, so a maximized window is filled instead of hosting a 1070px island between two ~650px voids. At ≥1560px the record grows to the lists' own --content-max (1240px), the rail takes 280px and the prose steps to 15px/82ch. At ≥1840px a record that has a contents rail (work, bug) becomes three columns — contents left of the document, fact card right, each sticky on its own — at 1780px, prose 16px/92ch, with the breadcrumb and head indented to the document column; the note page (no rail) grows to a 1440px two-column instead. scripts/screenshot.mjs's tall-shot CSS now also unsticks the side cards themselves, since in the three-column shape the stickiness sits on them rather than on .detail-side.

## Why

The owner reported it with two screenshots: the detail page balanced in a ~1430px window and adrift in the maximized 2533px one — the centred max-width block left voids wider than half the document on either side, and asked for the page to fill the window. A single centred column cannot gracefully exceed ~1450px with readable text, so the maximized step spends the width on a third column instead of on longer lines.

## How

CSS only, two media tiers at the end of app.css. The three-column shape is `display: contents` on .detail-side plus explicit grid placement, keyed on `:has(.side-card.contents)` so the DOM, the under-1100px collapse and the note page are untouched. Verified with Playwright shots at 2533/1900/1600/1428px (base unchanged at 1428), check-clipping and the check-i18n Korean word-integrity sweep both clean at 2533/1900/1600, and the canonical detail shots re-taken.

## For humans

The owner sent two screenshots of the same page: balanced in a window about 1430 pixels wide, adrift when it was maximized to 2533. The content sat in a centred strip, with an empty band down each side wider than half the strip. Fill the window, they asked.

**I widened the wrong page first.** In `src/styles/app.css`, the one stylesheet the app's layout comes from, I gave the record pages two wider steps. Those show one piece of work. The screenshots had been of the project dashboard, `/p/:id`, and the owner reported the same bands again.

**Then I raised the ceiling twice, and it was still a ceiling.** `--content-max` is the shared width limit the boards obey. I lifted it to about 1400 pixels, then to 1680. On a 2533-pixel window that still left some 340 pixels empty each side. In the end the dashboard and the boards got no limit at all: they run the full window.

**A document cannot simply fill the window.** Past roughly 1450 pixels a line of text gets hard to read: the eye loses its way back to the start of the next one. So a record keeps a measured width and spends extra room on a second column, not on longer lines.

It is like a newspaper: the sheet gets bigger, the columns do not.

**Pinning the contents list to the window edge tore the page into three.** The owner put it beside the earlier version: a column of text, a wide gap, then a thin strip at the edge. What reads best keeps the document and its contents list together as one block, which takes two sizes as the window grows.

**Shots at four widths, sweeps at three.** Playwright, a tool that drives an app the way a person does, took them at 2533, 1900, 1600 and 1428 pixels. The 1428 one was unchanged, so the ordinary window was not disturbed. At the three wide sizes `check-clipping`, which hunts for text cut off by the edge of its box, passed over 78 screens. `check-i18n` swept the same widths for words broken across a line.

Both sweeps are about text you cannot read; neither can say a page is well composed. That was the owner's own eye, four more rounds after the first.

A page that fills the window is not the same as a line you can still read.
