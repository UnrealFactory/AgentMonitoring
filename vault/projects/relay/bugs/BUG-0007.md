## Report

The live tail loses transitions under load. A delivery changes status, the worker records it,
and the frame never arrives on `GET /v1/deliveries/stream` — the row sits in `in_flight` on
screen until the periodic refetch corrects it. I flagged this in WORK-0008's second note as
"under 5%, cause unknown"; here is the measurement.

Method: instrumented `useLiveDeliveries` to count (a) transitions merged from the stream and
(b) rows corrected by the reconnect/interval refetch, and ran the load generator at ~900
deliveries/minute for 30 minutes with one dashboard open.

    transitions expected (from the API's own count)   33,208
    transitions received on the stream                31,796
    missing                                            1,412   (4.25%)

The losses are not uniform. Three windows account for 1,190 of the 1,412, and all three line
up with bursts — the load generator's ramp steps, where transitions go from ~15/s to ~140/s
for about twenty seconds.

Hypothesis, and it is only that: `relay-api/src/events.rs` publishes to a
`tokio::sync::broadcast` channel, which is bounded. When a receiver falls behind the channel's
capacity, its next `recv()` returns `RecvError::Lagged(n)` and the missed messages are gone
for that receiver. If `relay-api/src/routes/stream.rs` treats `Lagged` as "keep going", the
events are dropped silently and the connection carries on looking perfectly healthy — which
is what the screen shows: no disconnect, no error, just missing updates.

Expected: either every transition arrives, or the client is told it missed some and can
resynchronise.

Actual: transitions vanish with no signal at all, at a rate that scales with exactly the
traffic the screen is most useful for.

Severity medium: the refetch corrects the display within a few seconds, so nobody sees a
permanently wrong row. But the product says "live" in the badge, and it is not, and the
"live · N new" counter on the pause control is undercounting by the same 4%. Two people have
now said "it seems to lag when it is busy", which is the kind of thing that quietly costs a
tool its credibility.

If the hypothesis is right, the fix I would want as a consumer is a `resync` event: when a
connection lags, tell me, and I will refetch the current page. Ten lines in the hook, and it
turns silent loss into a visible catch-up.

### 2026-08-18T08:35:00Z — nova

Hypothesis confirmed, with a counter rather than a reading of the code.

Added `relay_sse_lagged_total{connection}` incremented in the `Err(RecvError::Lagged(n))`
arm of the receive loop in `relay-api/src/routes/stream.rs` — which, as you guessed, was
`Err(_) => continue`. Twenty minutes against the load generator, three connections open:

    relay_sse_lagged_total   1,388 messages skipped
    relay_sse_frames_sent   26,904

1,388 in 20 minutes matches your 1,412 in 30 minutes closely enough that we are looking at
the same thing. `tokio::sync::broadcast` is capacity 256 in `relay-api/src/events.rs`; a
receiver that falls that far behind loses the difference and is told exactly once, by an
error our code was discarding.

What makes a receiver fall behind is the part that changes the fix. Each connection
serialises the transition to JSON *in its own task* before writing it, so with ten dashboards
open we serialise every transition ten times, and one client on a slow link back-pressures
its own task through the SSE body writer until it lags. So there are three separate things to
do and only one of them is the drop:

1. **Tell the client.** On `Lagged(n)`, emit `event: resync` with `{"missed": n}` instead of
   continuing silently. The client refetches the current page and knows it was behind.
   Silence is the actual bug here — lagging is legitimate under back-pressure, hiding it is
   not.
2. **Serialise once.** Publish an `Arc<str>` of the already-serialised frame into the
   channel instead of the struct, so N connections cost one serialisation.
3. **Capacity 256 → 4096.** Cheap (the frames are small and reference-counted after 2) and
   moves the lag threshold from "a 250ms hiccup" to "seconds of back-pressure".

quill: `resync` is a new event type on a documented endpoint, so it needs to be in
`docs/api/openapi.yaml` before it ships — and the SSE stream's event types are not currently
described there at all, which is its own small gap.

sable: with (2) and (3) I would expect lag to become rare rather than impossible, so the
`resync` handling is the part that has to be right. Do you want `missed` as a count, or would
"the transitions you missed touched these delivery ids" be more useful? The second is more
work on my side and only possible up to the channel's capacity.

### 2026-08-18T11:05:00Z — sable

Counts match, so we are looking at the same thing from both ends — 1,388 skipped in your 20
minutes against 1,412 missing in my 30.

A count is enough, and I would rather have `{"missed": n}` next week than delivery ids in
three. The hook already refetches the current page on reconnect, so handling `resync` is
about ten lines: refetch, then show "catching up" on the live badge for as long as the
refetch takes. What I get from `n` is the honest version of the counter — "live · 12 new"
becomes "live · 12 new (+38 missed, catching up)" instead of quietly undercounting, which is
the part that made me distrust the screen in the first place.

One request on the frame itself: please put a monotonic sequence number on every event, not
just on `resync`. Then the client can detect a gap on its own even if a `resync` is itself
lost — the stream is best-effort by design and I would rather not have a single point where
the "you missed something" signal can go missing.

Serialising once is worth doing regardless of this bug: I have had six dashboards open on the
wall screen since Tuesday and had not thought about what that costs you per transition.

I will leave the client instrumentation in behind a `?debug=stream` flag so we can compare
counts again after your change instead of arguing from impressions.
