## Report

The retry ladder ignores `Retry-After`. An endpoint that rate-limits us and says exactly when
to come back gets its next request on our schedule instead of theirs, which is usually
sooner, which is how a rate limit becomes a block.

Found while writing `docs/guides/retries.md` for GA. I set out to document what we do with a
`429` and discovered that the answer is "the same thing we do with a 500", which is not a
sentence I can publish next to a competitor's guide promising the opposite.

Observed on staging, endpoint configured to return `429` with `Retry-After: 120`:

    12:03:11  attempt 1  429 Retry-After: 120
    12:03:21  attempt 2  429 Retry-After: 120     (ladder step 1: 10s)
    12:04:06  attempt 3  429 Retry-After: 120     (ladder step 2: 45s)
    12:07:06  attempt 4  429 Retry-After: 120     (ladder step 3: 3m)

Four requests inside the two-minute window the endpoint asked us to wait out, three of them
after it told us plainly not to. `relay-worker/src/retry.rs::next_attempt_at` takes the
attempt number and nothing else — the response headers are not passed to it at all, so this
is not a bug in the logic, it is a missing input. WORK-0005 lists it as follow-up 1, so it is
known; I am filing it because it has crossed from "known gap" into "blocks the guide, and
therefore GA".

Expected, and what I would like to be able to write:

- `Retry-After` is honoured on `429` and on `503`, in both its forms — delay-seconds
  (`Retry-After: 120`) and HTTP-date (`Retry-After: Wed, 19 Aug 2026 07:28:00 GMT`).
- The value is clamped: never earlier than the ladder step we would have used anyway (an
  endpoint cannot make us retry faster), never later than 24 hours (an endpoint cannot park a
  delivery indefinitely; beyond that the ladder wins and the delivery eventually dies
  honestly).
- The honoured value is recorded on the attempt, so the delivery log can say "waiting until
  12:05:11 because the endpoint asked" rather than showing a next-attempt time that appears
  to contradict our own documented ladder.
- Malformed values are ignored, not treated as zero. `Retry-After: soon` must not become an
  immediate retry.

Severity high: it is a correctness problem with the promise our documentation is about to
make, and it actively makes things worse for the customers it hits — endpoints that
rate-limit are endpoints under load, and we are adding to it. There is no workaround a
customer can apply from their side other than returning a status we back off from more
slowly, which is the wrong lesson to teach.

Not claiming this: it is worker logic and I would get the clamping wrong. Happy to write the
guide section the moment the behaviour is decided, and to review the wording of whatever the
delivery log shows.
