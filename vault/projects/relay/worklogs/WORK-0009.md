## What

Let a customer change an endpoint's signing secret without a synchronised deploy:

- `relay-store/migrations/0007_secret_rotation.sql` — `endpoints.secret_previous`,
  `endpoints.secret_rotated_at`, `endpoints.secret_overlap_hours` (default 24).
- `POST /v1/endpoints/{id}/rotate-secret` (`relay-api/src/routes/endpoints.rs`) — generates
  the new secret, moves the current one to `secret_previous`, stamps the rotation time, and
  returns the plaintext **once**.
- `relay-core/src/signature.rs::sign_all(secrets, ts, body)` — returns one signature per
  live secret; `relay-worker/src/http_client.rs` joins them into
  `t=<unix>,v1=<sig-current>,v1=<sig-previous>`.
- `relay-worker/src/secrets.rs` — a sweep every five minutes that clears `secret_previous`
  once the overlap window has passed, and the `relay_endpoints_dual_signing` gauge.
- `scripts/rotate-secret.sh` for operators; the dashboard button is sable's, separately.

## Why

Rotating a secret today means the customer changes it on their side at the same instant we
change it on ours. Nobody can do that, so nobody rotates, so a secret that appeared in a log
file in March is still signing traffic in August. The overlap window turns an atomic
coordination problem into a deploy the customer can do on Tuesday afternoon: rotate, both
signatures are sent for 24 hours, deploy the new secret whenever, the old one stops working
on its own.

Rejected: **two permanently valid secrets**. It removes the deadline, and the leaked secret
we were rotating away from stays valid forever, which is the entire thing we are trying to
end. Rejected: **rotate by creating a replacement endpoint**. The endpoint id is what
customers key their own configuration and our support conversations on, and both endpoints
would receive every event during the migration — duplicate deliveries as a feature.

The overlap is per endpoint (`secret_overlap_hours`) rather than global because the range of
customers is real: one runs a deploy in eight minutes, another needs a change-advisory board
and a week.

## How

Secrets have to be recoverable to be signed with, so they cannot be hashed like an API key.
`secret_current` and `secret_previous` are `bytea`, sealed with libsodium under a key from
`RELAY_SECRET_KEY` (env, injected from the cluster secret store), decrypted per delivery
inside the worker and never logged; `Endpoint`'s `Debug` impl is hand-written to print
`secret_current: <redacted>`, because the derived one printed it into a tracing span the
first time I looked.

Signature order is current first, previous second, and that ordering is deliberately *not*
load-bearing: quill's rule in `docs/guides/signing.md` is "accept if any `vN` matches, in
constant time", and a verifier that only checks the first signature breaks during a rotation
whichever order we choose. The `v0`/`v1` window from BUG-0002 already shipped the
multi-signature header form, so customers integrating this week are writing the loop
anyway — that sequencing was quill's argument on that thread and it is paying off here.

We cannot see a customer's verification failures, but we can see their consequences: an
endpoint that starts returning `401`/`403` shortly after a rotation is almost certainly a
verifier that only checks one signature. The rotation sweep flags endpoints whose 4xx rate
rises inside their overlap window, which is the alert someone can act on before the window
closes and the failure becomes permanent.

## Updates

### 2026-08-11T13:45:00Z

Rotation endpoint and dual signing work end to end: rotate, capture a delivery, and the
header carries two `v1` values that verify against the new and the old secret respectively.

`Endpoint`'s derived `Debug` printed `secret_current` into a tracing span the first time I
looked at a rotation trace — a plaintext signing secret in the log pipeline, retained for 30
days. Hand-written `Debug` now prints `<redacted>` for both secret fields, and
`relay-core/tests/no_secret_in_debug.rs` asserts the rendered string contains neither the
plaintext nor its hex, so a future derive cannot bring it back quietly.

The expiry sweep is deliberately in the worker rather than in a cron: the worker is already a
long-lived process with a database connection and a five-minute tick, and adding a second
kind of deployable to run one query is how you end up with a job nobody has looked at in a
year.

### 2026-08-12T15:20:00Z

Rehearsed a full rotation against a receiver that verifies signatures, which turned up the
one thing the design could not tell me on paper.

Sequence: rotate, receiver keeps the old secret, deliveries keep verifying (both signatures
sent); receiver switches to the new secret, deliveries keep verifying; sweep clears
`secret_previous` after the window; a receiver still on the old secret starts returning 401,
as it should.

The gap is what happens in that last state. We know the endpoint is failing — 401s on the
delivery — but nobody is told *why*, and "your endpoint returned 401" is not a useful
sentence when the cause is a rotation the customer started 24 hours ago and forgot. The
sweep now records `rotation_completed_at` and the delivery-failure path checks it: a 401 or
403 within an hour of a window closing is labelled `likely_stale_secret` on the attempt and
surfaced in the dashboard's error text. Cheap, and it turns a support thread into a sentence.

Also confirmed the overlap is genuinely per endpoint: setting `secret_overlap_hours = 168`
on one endpoint and 1 on another produced exactly the expected sweeps, and the
`relay_endpoints_dual_signing` gauge shows how many endpoints are mid-rotation at any moment,
which is the number an operator wants before deploying anything to the signing path.

## Outcome

Endpoint secrets can be rotated without a synchronised deploy, and the customer's side of it
is a change they can make whenever they get to it.

Shipped:

- `relay-store/migrations/0007_secret_rotation.sql` — `secret_previous`,
  `secret_rotated_at`, `secret_overlap_hours` (default 24), `rotation_completed_at`.
- `POST /v1/endpoints/{id}/rotate-secret` — returns the new secret once, moves the current
  one to `secret_previous`, starts the window. Documented in `docs/api/openapi.yaml` with
  the "you will never see this value again" warning in the response description.
- `relay-core/src/signature.rs::sign_all` and
  `relay-worker/src/http_client.rs` — the header carries one `v1` per live secret,
  current first.
- `relay-worker/src/secrets.rs` — five-minute sweep that clears `secret_previous` when the
  window closes and stamps `rotation_completed_at`; `relay_endpoints_dual_signing` gauge.
- `scripts/rotate-secret.sh`; `docs/guides/signing.md`'s rotation section is now true rather
  than forward-looking.

Verified:

- Full rehearsal against a verifying receiver: rotate → receiver on old secret still
  verifies → receiver switches to new secret, still verifies → sweep clears the old one →
  a receiver left on the old secret starts failing, as designed. Per-endpoint windows of 1h
  and 168h both swept at the right moment.
- `cargo test --workspace` — 68 passed, including `sign_all_emits_one_signature_per_secret`,
  the sweep boundary test, and `no_secret_in_debug`.
- `relay-core/tests/no_secret_in_debug.rs` exists because the derived `Debug` printed
  `secret_current` in plaintext into a tracing span the first time I looked at a rotation
  trace — a signing secret in the log pipeline with 30-day retention. Both secret fields now
  render `<redacted>` and the test asserts neither the plaintext nor its hex appears.

The part I would not have got from the design: a customer who never finishes the rotation
just gets 401s a day later with no explanation. The sweep records `rotation_completed_at`,
and a 401/403 within an hour of a window closing is now labelled `likely_stale_secret` on the
attempt and shown in the dashboard's error text. That turns a support thread into a sentence.

Sequencing worth noting: this works because BUG-0002's fix already shipped a header carrying
two signatures. Customers integrating this week are writing "accept if any `vN` matches" from
`docs/guides/signing.md` because they have to, not because we asked them to prepare for a
rotation they have not done yet. That was quill's argument on BUG-0002 and it saved us a
second migration.

Follow-ups: the dashboard has no rotate button yet (sable's, and it needs a
"copy this now, you will not see it again" flow that is worth doing properly); and rotation
is per endpoint only — a tenant-wide rotation is a loop over endpoints today, which is fine
at 900 endpoints and not at 90,000.
