## What

Make the delivery pipeline visible, then let it size itself:

- `relay-core/src/telemetry.rs` — `tracing` subscriber with OTLP export. A trace starts at
  `POST /v1/events`, the `traceparent` is stored on the delivery row (`deliveries.trace_id`,
  migration `0009_trace_id.sql`), each attempt is a span under it, and the header is
  forwarded to the customer's endpoint so their trace joins ours.
- `/metrics` on both binaries: `relay_delivery_attempts_total{outcome}`,
  `relay_delivery_duration_seconds` (explicit buckets to 30s),
  `relay_queue_depth{status}`, `relay_queue_oldest_pending_seconds`,
  `relay_db_connections{state}`, and `relay_signatures_emitted_total{version}` from
  BUG-0002.
- `deploy/k8s/worker-deployment.yaml` — the worker as a plain Deployment (2–12 replicas),
  carrying the graceful-shutdown parts of the abandoned StatefulSet: 45s termination grace,
  preStop drain.
- `deploy/k8s/keda-scaledobject.yaml` — KEDA's Postgres scaler on oldest-pending age and
  queue depth.
- `deploy/grafana/relay-delivery.json` and `deploy/alerts/relay.yaml`.

## Why

BUG-0004 took 29 minutes to diagnose, and 25 of those were spent discovering that the only
place the truth existed was `pg_stat_activity` in a psql session someone had to open by
hand. Every number in that incident — connections parked in a transaction, queue depth
climbing, oldest delivery ageing — is a number the system knows and does not report.

Scaling on CPU is worse than not scaling. A worker waiting on a slow endpoint is using no
CPU while the backlog grows, so CPU-based autoscaling scales *down* exactly when the system
is furthest behind. The quantity the on-call actually cares about, and the one the SLO is
written in, is "how long has the oldest due delivery been waiting" — so that is what the
autoscaler reads.

Rejected: scaling on queue depth alone. Depth is a fine signal until a burst of 40k
deliveries arrives that will drain in ninety seconds on the current replicas, and the
autoscaler doubles the pods for nothing. Oldest-pending age answers "are we keeping up",
which is the actual question; depth stays as a secondary trigger with a much higher
threshold for the case where age is low because nothing has come due yet.

## How

Traces cross the process boundary through the database, not through a message bus: the
ingest handler serialises its `traceparent` into `deliveries.trace_id`, and the worker
restores it as the parent of the attempt span. That is the only place the two processes
meet, and it means a delivery retried 31 hours later still hangs off the request that
created it.

Histogram buckets are explicit: 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 30, +Inf. The
default Prometheus buckets stop at 10s, which is the wrong side of our 30s client timeout —
the interesting population is exactly the deliveries between 10s and the timeout, and
default buckets render all of them as one indistinguishable `+Inf` blob.

KEDA over a raw HPA with custom metrics, because the query is the configuration: the scaler
runs `SELECT extract(epoch from now() - min(next_attempt_at)) FROM deliveries WHERE status =
'pending' AND next_attempt_at <= now()` every 30s. Scale-up is immediate, scale-down waits
out a 300s cooldown and a 120s stabilisation window, because worker startup costs a
connection-pool warm-up and flapping replicas during a retry ladder would be its own
incident.

The alerts are deliberately few — oldest-pending over 5 minutes, dead-letter rate over 1% of
attempts in 15 minutes, a rotation overlap window closing within 4 hours on an endpoint
whose 4xx rate has risen, and connection-pool saturation over 80%. Every one of them names a
runbook section. An alert nobody can act on is a pager that gets ignored, which costs more
than not having it.

## Updates

### 2026-08-13T14:50:00Z

Traces run end to end: `POST /v1/events` to the attempt span to the customer's endpoint, with
the `traceparent` carried across the process boundary in `deliveries.trace_id`. Opening one
delivery in Grafana and seeing the ingest request that created it 31 hours earlier is worth
the whole afternoon.

First thing the traces said, which nobody would have guessed from the code: 61% of the median
delivery's wall time was TLS handshake. `relay-worker/src/http_client.rs` built a
`reqwest::Client` per attempt, so the connection pool was reconstructed every time and no
connection was ever reused. One client built at startup and cloned — `Client` is already an
`Arc` internally, which is exactly why this is easy to get wrong — took median delivery
latency from 148ms to 51ms. nova took the two-line change; I am recording it here because the
instrumentation is what found it, and "the profiler is a trace" is the argument for doing
this before the next optimisation, not after.

Metrics are exported from both binaries and scraped. `relay_db_connections{state}` would have
shown BUG-0004 in one panel: `idle_in_transaction` climbing to the pool size and staying
there.

### 2026-08-15T11:20:00Z

KEDA is live in staging and the scaling test is convincing. Setup: 40,000 deliveries queued
in one burst against endpoints that respond in ~200ms, starting from 2 replicas.

    t+0s     burst arrives, oldest-pending age 0s, depth 40k
    t+30s    scaler polls: age 34s > threshold 30s -> 2 -> 6 replicas
    t+52s    pods ready, drain rate 4,100/min -> 11,800/min
    t+90s    scaler polls: age 41s, still above -> 6 -> 9 replicas
    t+4m10s  queue drained, age 0s
    t+9m10s  cooldown (300s) elapsed, scale down begins
    t+11m    back to 2 replicas

Scale-up on age rather than depth is the right call for the reason I expected: a second test
queued 40k deliveries that were all `next_attempt_at` in the future (a retry ladder step), so
depth was 40k and nothing was late. The depth trigger alone would have scaled to 12 replicas
to watch a queue that had no work due; the age trigger correctly did nothing.

Also tuned the alerts against real data rather than intuition: the dead-letter alert at 1% of
attempts over 15 minutes fired twice in three days, both times legitimately (a misconfigured
staging endpoint returning 404 on every delivery), so the threshold stays. The
connection-pool alert at 80% has not fired since BUG-0004's fix, which is the point.

### 2026-08-17T16:45:00Z

Runbooks are written and linked from every alert, which was the condition I set for the
alerts existing at all: `docs/operations/runbooks/queue-backlog.md`,
`dead-letter-spike.md`, `pool-saturation.md`, `rotation-window.md`. Each one opens with the
three commands to run and the Grafana panel that answers the question, not with prose.

The Grafana dashboard (`deploy/grafana/relay-delivery.json`) is provisioned from the repo
rather than clicked together in the UI, so it survives the next cluster and shows up in code
review. Six panels: delivery outcomes over time, queue depth by status, oldest-pending age
with the alert threshold drawn on it, delivery duration heat map, connection pool by state,
and dead letters by endpoint.

The oldest-pending panel proved itself yesterday: a staging endpoint with an expired TLS
certificate produced a flat 40-minute-old oldest-pending line while depth stayed low, which
is a shape you cannot see in a depth graph at all. Diagnosed from the panel in about ninety
seconds; before this work it would have been a psql session and a guess.

Left before this is done: replica count bounds want one more look under a real burst, and I
want the KEDA cooldown checked against a full 24h ladder step rather than the synthetic
tests.

## Outcome

The delivery pipeline reports on itself and sizes itself. Both halves are in production and
have each already earned their keep once.

Shipped:

- `relay-core/src/telemetry.rs` — OTLP traces from `POST /v1/events` through the delivery
  attempt to the customer's endpoint, joined across processes by `deliveries.trace_id`
  (migration `0009_trace_id.sql`) and forwarded as `traceparent` so a customer's trace
  connects to ours.
- `/metrics` on both binaries: `relay_delivery_attempts_total{outcome}`,
  `relay_delivery_duration_seconds` (explicit buckets to 30s),
  `relay_queue_depth{status}`, `relay_queue_oldest_pending_seconds`,
  `relay_db_connections{state}`, `relay_signatures_emitted_total{version}`,
  `relay_endpoints_dual_signing`.
- `deploy/k8s/worker-deployment.yaml` — plain Deployment, 2–12 replicas, 45s termination
  grace and a preStop drain (the parts of the abandoned WORK-0007 StatefulSet worth keeping).
- `deploy/k8s/keda-scaledobject.yaml` — KEDA Postgres scaler on oldest-pending age
  (threshold 30s) with queue depth as a secondary trigger, 300s cooldown, 120s scale-down
  stabilisation.
- `deploy/grafana/relay-delivery.json` — six panels, provisioned from the repository.
- `deploy/alerts/relay.yaml` plus four runbooks under `docs/operations/runbooks/`.

Measured:

- **Scaling**, 40,000-delivery burst from 2 replicas: scaled 2 → 6 at t+30s, 6 → 9 at t+90s,
  drained at t+4m10s, back to 2 at t+11m. Drain rate went 4,100/min → 11,800/min.
- **Age, not depth**, is the right trigger: a control test queued 40,000 deliveries whose
  `next_attempt_at` was in the future. Depth said 40k and would have scaled to 12; age said
  0s and correctly did nothing.
- **Traces paid for themselves in an afternoon**: 61% of median delivery wall time was TLS
  handshake, because `relay-worker/src/http_client.rs` built a `reqwest::Client` per attempt
  and never reused a connection. One client built at startup and cloned took median delivery
  latency from 148ms to 51ms. No profiler, no theory — the trace waterfall just showed it.
- **The dashboard found a real fault before a human did**: a staging endpoint with an expired
  certificate held oldest-pending flat at 40 minutes while depth stayed low — a shape that is
  invisible in a depth graph. Diagnosed in about ninety seconds.
- `cargo test --workspace` — 79 passed. `kubectl apply --dry-run=server` clean for all
  manifests; alert rules validated with `promtool check rules`.

Deliberately few alerts, four, each naming a runbook section: oldest-pending over 5 minutes,
dead letters over 1% of attempts in 15 minutes, connection pool over 80%, and a rotation
window closing on an endpoint whose 4xx rate has risen. The dead-letter alert fired twice in
three days, both times correctly. The pool alert has not fired since BUG-0004 was fixed,
which is the whole point of it existing.

Follow-ups: replica bounds (2–12) are guesses that have not met a real burst yet, only
synthetic ones; the KEDA cooldown has not been observed across a full 24h ladder step; and
traces are sampled at 100% today, which is fine at current volume and will not be at ten
times it — the decision to sample belongs with the first customer whose traffic makes it
necessary, not now.
