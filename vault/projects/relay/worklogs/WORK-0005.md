## What

Turn "it failed" into a schedule and an end state:

- `relay-worker/src/retry.rs` — `classify(&Outcome) -> Disposition` (retry or terminal) and
  `next_attempt_at(attempt_no) -> Option<OffsetDateTime>` over a fixed ladder: 10s, 45s,
  3m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h. Eight attempts, the last one about 31 hours after the first.
- ±20% uniform jitter on every step.
- `relay-store/migrations/0005_dead_letters.sql` — a `dead_letters` table and the `dead`
  value on `deliveries.status`.
- `relay-worker/src/dlq.rs` — moves a delivery whose ladder is exhausted into `dead_letters`
  with its last status, last error and attempt count.
- `relay-api/src/routes/deliveries.rs` — `POST /v1/deliveries/{id}/replay` creates a *new*
  delivery row with `replay_of` pointing at the original, and returns `202` with the new id.

## Why

Endpoints are down for minutes to hours, not seconds. A fixed 60s retry is wrong in both
directions at once: it gives up long before a customer finishes a deploy, and while the
endpoint is genuinely dead it sends the same request sixty times an hour to something that
is not listening. The ladder is short at the start (a rolling deploy is usually over inside
a minute) and long at the end (an expired TLS certificate on a Friday is not fixed before
Monday, and 24h steps mean we are still trying when someone gets to it).

Jitter is not decoration. Endpoint outages are correlated by construction: every delivery
for an endpoint fails in the same second, so every retry is scheduled for the same second,
and the endpoint's first breath after recovery gets the entire backlog at once. Measured on
the synthetic outage below: 4,300 deliveries for one endpoint became due within the same
900ms window. ±20% spreads them across minutes instead.

Rejected: a `2^n` formula. The early steps are too tight to survive a deploy (1s, 2s, 4s all
land inside the same restart) and the late ones drift past a day in a way nobody can quote
in a support conversation. A ladder is a table a customer can read and plan around, which is
worth more than the elegance.

Also rejected: retrying forever with an age cap instead of a dead-letter state. "Still
retrying, 4 days in" is not information anyone can act on; `dead` with a reason is.

## How

The ladder is a `const [Duration; 7]`, not a computation, with a `debug_assert` that it is
strictly ascending. `classify` is the interesting half:

- `408`, `429` and any `5xx` → retry;
- connection, DNS, TLS and timeout errors → retry;
- `410 Gone` → terminal immediately, no ladder — that is the response an endpoint gives when
  it has been decommissioned, and retrying it for 31 hours is pure noise;
- other `4xx` → terminal. The endpoint understood the request and refused it; a retry sends
  the identical bytes and gets the identical refusal.
- `2xx`/`3xx` other than the success range are recorded verbatim; only `2xx` counts as
  delivered.

Jitter is applied when the next attempt is scheduled, not when it is claimed, so the spread
is visible in `next_attempt_at` and the dashboard can show a real time rather than "soon".

Replay creates a new delivery instead of resetting the old one. Resetting would rewrite
history — the attempt timeline of the original delivery is evidence in a support
conversation, and a replay that overwrote it would delete the reason someone asked for the
replay. `replay_of` makes the relationship visible in both directions, and it means a replay
is subject to the same ladder as any other delivery, with no special case in the worker.

## Updates

### 2026-08-04T12:10:00Z

Ladder and classification are in, with the numbers from the synthetic outage that justify
the jitter.

Setup: 4,300 pending deliveries for one endpoint, receiver returns 503 for three hours, then
200. Without jitter, 4,300 deliveries came due inside the same 900ms window every step of
the way, and the recovery burst was 4,300 requests in under a second — enough to knock over
the receiver a second time, which is the failure mode where our retry policy becomes the
customer's outage. With ±20%, the same backlog spreads over 4m48s at the 24h step and the
receiver never exceeded 40 concurrent requests.

`classify` treats `410 Gone` as terminal with no ladder. That came out of the staging data:
an endpoint deleted on the customer side answers `410` for every one of the 8 attempts, and
31 hours of that is 8 pointless requests per delivery for something that has told us
plainly that it is gone.

Next: the dead-letter table and the replay endpoint.

### 2026-08-05T16:35:00Z

Dead-letter path and replay are in.

`dlq.rs` moves a delivery whose ladder is exhausted into `dead_letters` with its attempt
count, last response status and last error, sets `deliveries.status = 'dead'`, and does both
in one transaction so a row can never be dead in one place and pending in the other.

Replay creates a new delivery row with `replay_of` set, rather than resetting the original.
Resetting was the first draft and it is wrong: the attempt timeline on the original delivery
is the evidence someone read before deciding to replay, and rewriting it deletes the reason
the replay happened. The new row also goes through the normal ladder with no special case in
the worker.

`scripts/drain-dlq.sh` replays a whole endpoint's dead letters with a concurrency cap, for
the case that produces most of them: an endpoint that was misconfigured for six hours and is
now fixed. It prints what it would do without `--yes`, because the first version of this
script in every product I have seen has replayed a million deliveries by accident.

## Outcome

Failed deliveries now have a schedule, a spread, and an end.

Shipped:

- `relay-worker/src/retry.rs` — the ladder (10s, 45s, 3m, 15m, 1h, 6h, 24h; eight attempts
  ending ~31h after the first) as a `const` table with ±20% uniform jitter, and `classify`,
  which separates retryable from terminal: `408`/`429`/`5xx`, timeouts, DNS, TLS and connect
  errors retry; `410 Gone` is terminal immediately; other `4xx` are terminal.
- `relay-store/migrations/0005_dead_letters.sql` — the `dead_letters` table and the `dead`
  status.
- `relay-worker/src/dlq.rs` — exhausted deliveries move to `dead_letters` with attempt
  count, last status and last error, in the same transaction that sets `status = 'dead'`.
- `relay-api/src/routes/deliveries.rs` — `POST /v1/deliveries/{id}/replay` creates a new
  delivery with `replay_of` set and returns `202`.
- `scripts/drain-dlq.sh` — bulk replay for one endpoint with a concurrency cap; prints the
  plan unless `--yes` is passed.

Measured, on the synthetic outage (4,300 pending deliveries for one endpoint, receiver
returns 503 for three hours then 200):

- without jitter, all 4,300 came due inside the same 900ms window at every step, and
  recovery was 4,300 requests in under a second — our retry policy becoming the customer's
  second outage;
- with ±20%, the same backlog spreads over 4m48s at the 24h step and the receiver never saw
  more than 40 concurrent requests;
- `cargo test --workspace` — 58 passed, including a ladder test that asserts strict
  ascent and total span, and `dlq_moves_exhausted_delivery_once` running two workers against
  the same exhausted delivery.
- Drain script dry run against staging's 61 dead letters printed 61 planned replays and
  created nothing.

Design note for whoever reads the schema and wonders: replay creates a *new* delivery row
rather than resetting the original, because the original's attempt timeline is the evidence
someone read before deciding to replay. Rewriting it would delete the reason the replay
happened, and the support screen would then show a delivery that had apparently never
failed.

Follow-ups, none of them hidden:

1. **`Retry-After` is ignored.** A `429` goes on the fixed ladder like any other retryable
   failure, so an endpoint that tells us "come back in 90 seconds" gets a request in 45.
   Honouring it is a small change to `next_attempt_at` and a bigger question about capping
   what we will accept.
2. **No per-endpoint circuit breaker.** An endpoint that is down still gets every one of its
   deliveries attempted on schedule; a breaker would let us skip straight to the long steps.
3. **Attempt rows accumulate.** Eight per failed delivery, and nothing prunes them.
