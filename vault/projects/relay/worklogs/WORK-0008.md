## What

Stop polling the delivery log and stream it:

- `relay-api/src/routes/stream.rs` — `GET /v1/deliveries/stream`, a Server-Sent Events
  endpoint that emits one `delivery` event per status transition, filtered by the same query
  parameters as the list (`endpoint`, `status`).
- `relay-api/src/events.rs` — an in-process `tokio::sync::broadcast` channel that the write
  paths publish transitions to, so the stream costs one subscriber per connection and no
  extra database load.
- `dashboard/src/lib/useLiveDeliveries.ts` — the hook: subscribes, merges transitions into
  the TanStack Query cache for the current filter, reconnects with backoff, and exposes a
  connection state the UI can show.
- `dashboard/src/routes/DeliveryLog.tsx` — rows update in place, with a "live" indicator and
  a pause control.

## Why

The log polls every five seconds today, which is wrong in both of the ways a support
engineer notices. Watching a delivery that is retrying means staring at a table that changes
under your cursor at an arbitrary moment — the row you were about to click moves. And five
seconds is long enough that a customer on a call says "I just triggered it" and the screen
disagrees with them, which is the exact moment the tool loses their trust.

SSE over WebSockets: this is one-directional, it survives every proxy that speaks HTTP/1.1,
and `EventSource` reconnects on its own with `Last-Event-ID`. A WebSocket would give us a
channel back that we have no use for, plus a keepalive protocol to write and a proxy
configuration to explain to whoever deploys this.

Rejected: dropping the poll entirely and rendering only from the stream. A stream is a
best-effort view of transitions; the list endpoint is the truth. The hook merges into the
existing cache so a missed event is corrected by the next fetch instead of leaving the
screen permanently wrong.

## How

Server side, transitions are published to a `broadcast` channel from
`relay-store::queue::release` and the ingest path, and each SSE connection subscribes with
its own receiver. Serialisation happens once per transition, not once per connection.
Comment frames go out every 15s as a keepalive, because idle proxies close a quiet
connection at 30 or 60 seconds and the reconnect storm that follows is worse than the
bytes.

The client side is where the care goes. The hook does not append rows blindly: a transition
is merged into the page only if it matches the current filter, and a delivery that no longer
matches (it was `pending`, you are filtering `pending`, it just succeeded) is removed with a
250ms fade rather than vanishing mid-read. Rows never reorder while the pointer is inside
the table — reordering is deferred until `mouseleave` or the next pagination, because a
table that reshuffles under the cursor makes people misclick and stop trusting it.

The pause control freezes merging without closing the connection, and the indicator counts
what accumulated while paused ("live · 12 new"), so pausing to read is not the same as
falling behind silently.

Reconnect is exponential with jitter, capped at 30s, and on reconnect the hook refetches the
current page rather than trusting `Last-Event-ID` to have replayed everything — the
broadcast channel has a bounded buffer and does not promise history.

## Updates

### 2026-08-08T15:05:00Z

The stream is up: `GET /v1/deliveries/stream` publishes from the broadcast channel and
`useLiveDeliveries` merges transitions into the query cache. Watching a retry ladder play out
in the log without a refresh is the first time this product has felt like a monitoring tool
rather than a table.

Two things the first version got wrong and now handles. Idle proxies were closing the
connection at 60s of quiet, and `EventSource` reconnects instantly, so an idle dashboard
produced a reconnect every minute for every open tab; a 15s comment frame keepalive stopped
that dead. And rows were reordering under the pointer whenever a transition changed the sort
key, which made me misclick twice in five minutes — merging now defers reordering until
`mouseleave` or the next page change, so the row you are aiming at stays where it is.

Median time from a worker recording a transition to the row repainting: 180ms, against 2.5s
average (5s worst case) on the poll.

### 2026-08-10T17:40:00Z

Pause control and reconnect are in, and the log has been open on the wall screen all
afternoon without a refresh.

Pause freezes merging without closing the stream, and the indicator counts what is waiting
("live · 12 new") so pausing to read a row is visibly different from the stream having
died — the failure I was most worried about is a dashboard that looks live and is not.
Reconnect is exponential with jitter to a 30s cap, and on every reconnect the hook refetches
the current page rather than trusting the stream to have replayed anything.

That refetch is doing more work than I expected. Under the load generator at ~900
deliveries/minute, a handful of transitions never arrive on the stream — a row sits in
`in_flight` on screen until the next refetch corrects it. It is under 5% and the correction
makes it invisible in normal use, but it means the stream is lossy under load and I do not
yet know why. Instrumenting it before I make claims: if it is the broadcast channel's
bounded buffer, that is a server-side fix and a real bug, not a UI concern.

## Outcome

The delivery log is live: transitions arrive over SSE and rows update in place, no polling,
no refresh.

Shipped:

- `relay-api/src/routes/stream.rs` — `GET /v1/deliveries/stream`, SSE, filtered by the same
  parameters as the list endpoint, 15s comment-frame keepalive.
- `relay-api/src/events.rs` — one `tokio::sync::broadcast` channel published to by the write
  paths; each connection is a subscriber, and a transition is serialised once regardless of
  how many dashboards are open.
- `dashboard/src/lib/useLiveDeliveries.ts` — subscribe, filter, merge into the TanStack
  Query cache, reconnect with jittered backoff to a 30s cap, refetch the page on reconnect.
- `dashboard/src/routes/DeliveryLog.tsx` — live indicator, pause control, in-place row
  updates, removal with a 250ms fade when a row stops matching the filter.

Measured: median transition-to-repaint 180ms, against 2.5s average and 5s worst case on the
poll. Ten dashboards open against one API pod add 0.4% CPU and no database load, which is
the point of publishing from a broadcast channel rather than polling per connection.

Verified: `cargo test -p relay-api` — 24 passed, including a test that opens a stream,
performs a transition and asserts the frame arrives with the right shape.
`vitest run` — 46 passed, including merge-into-cache, filter-out-on-transition and the
reconnect backoff schedule. `npx playwright test dashboard/e2e/live-tail.spec.ts` — a
delivery driven through `pending → in_flight → succeeded` appears on screen without a
refetch. Left the log open on the wall screen for six hours: no reconnect loops, no memory
growth (heap flat at 41MB after six hours and ~30k merged transitions).

Two behaviours that look like polish and are not: rows do not reorder while the pointer is
inside the table (deferred to `mouseleave`), because a table that reshuffles under the cursor
makes people misclick; and pausing keeps the connection open and counts what is waiting
("live · 12 new"), so "I paused" is visibly different from "the stream died".

Known and now filed: under sustained load the stream loses a small fraction of transitions —
a row sits in `in_flight` on screen until the next refetch corrects it. The reconnect refetch
hides it in normal use, but the stream is lossy and the cause is server-side. Measured
properly and filed as its own bug rather than buried here.
