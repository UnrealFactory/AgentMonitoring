## What

The second pass over the project dashboard, against the two gaps the P4 critics ended round 1
with: one about what the screen *says* (a reader could not tell which open bug was the high one,
or that a shipping control had a known data-loss path), and one about what the charts *label*
(a week of data with no date anywhere on the axis).

Six changes, in `src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx`, `src/lib/dashboard.ts`,
`src/components/charts.tsx` and `src/styles/app.css`:

- **Open bugs are listed, not only counted.** One row per open bug — status dot, title,
  severity pill, id, the assignee or `unclaimed`, how long it has been open, and whether
  anything has happened to it since it was filed — in triage order: worst first, and inside one
  severity the unowned before the owned. The severity chips stay above them for the whole
  distribution, zeros included.
- **The newest note of every in-flight work log is quoted**, as the opening sentence of each of
  its paragraphs (the first two and the last, when there are more), each on its own line.
- **Liveness is measured from the clock.** The LIVE flag now means "something was recorded in
  the last two hours"; an in-flight row whose newest note is hours old carries a hollow ring and
  the words "no update in 11h", and one with no notes at all says "no updates yet" instead of
  dressing its own start time up as an update.
- **Every axis carries its dates.** Ticks land on natural boundaries (00:00, 06:00, 12:00 …,
  or whole days) and each one that opens a new UTC day prints that date under it. The page says
  UTC in three places on its face rather than only inside `title` attributes.
- **The range control changes the charts' numbers.** Each legend entry carries the change inside
  the window (`+3`, `−1`, `±0`) beside the running total, and the first bucket is clipped back to
  the requested start so those figures are the vault's figures.
- **The activity feed opens on time, not on volume**, its collapsed day bars are as long as the
  day was busy and carry a legend, and the day's cast is drawn as avatars rather than a list of
  names cut mid-name.

## Why

A dashboard is judged by what a reader who was not watching can say after thirty seconds on it.
Round 1's reader wrote an accurate brief and still missed the single most alarming fact in the
relay project: `BUG-0008` is a high-severity GA blocker, filed by quill, that nobody has claimed
and nobody has touched. The card said "2 still open of 8 filed" and drew four anonymous chips
(Critical 0 · High 1 · Medium 1 · Low 0); the only bug it named was the *oldest*, which is the
medium one nova had already claimed and written a fix plan into. Counting a severity is not the
same as binding it to a bug, an owner and an id, and "unclaimed" was reachable only by noticing
that a `bug_claimed` line was *absent* from the feed — a conclusion drawn from missing evidence,
on the fact that most needs stating.

The same reader reported WORK-0012 as clean progress. The vault's own event summary for its
newest note stops after the first clause; the rest of that note says pause is wired but a paused
endpoint keeps advancing the retry ladder, so anything paused beyond ~31 hours exhausts into
`dead_letters` with no request ever sent — a data-loss path from a button labelled "protect this
endpoint" — and that sable has two questions to nova outstanding. Nothing on the page
distinguished "in flight" from "in flight and waiting on somebody".

And the page said LIVE, with two blue "working now" dots, over an event log whose newest line was
ten hours old.

The charts had the mirror-image problem: honest numbers, unreadable labels. Because the bucket
size was derived from the step rather than the span, a seven-day chart was labelled
"12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00 · 00:00 · 12:00", and a four-day-old project labelled its default view
"06:00 · 00:00 · 18:00 · 12:00 · 06:00" — a descending sequence that reads as a broken axis.
play.grafana.org at Last 7 days prints "08/13 00:00". A reader could not tell what period the
chart covered. Filed as BUG-0011, with the legend-not-following-the-range defect as BUG-0012.

## How

**Triage order, not creation order** (`triageOrder` in `src/lib/dashboard.ts`): severity rank,
then unowned before owned, then oldest first. The panel prints the first three and links the
rest to the board. `unclaimed` is rendered as an outline chip where an owned bug wears its
assignee's avatar, so ownership is a state on the row rather than an inference from an absence.

**The note outline** (`noteOutline`): the newest update is split on blank lines, fenced and
indented code is dropped, and each remaining paragraph contributes its opening sentence. When
there are more paragraphs than there is room for, the *last* one is kept in place of the middle
and the row marks the skip with a leading `…` — a note opens with what happened and closes with
what is still outstanding, and the outstanding half is the one a dashboard exists to surface.
The strip reads the record itself (`api.getWorklog`) for the handful of logs that are open,
never more than the three rows it prints, because `events.jsonl` only carries the truncated
clause.

**Freshness** (`freshness`): live ≤ 2h, stale ≥ 24h, quiet in between. It drives the header flag,
the dots on the in-flight rows, the dots in the Agents table and the words beside each duration.
Every dot's meaning is also written in words on the same row, so nothing is carried by colour.

**Ticks** (`axisTicks`): the interval is the smallest natural one — 1/2/3/6/12 hours, 1/2/3/7/14/28
days — that is a whole number of buckets and leaves 64px between labels, and a tick may only land
on a boundary of that interval. Sub-daily ticks therefore read 00:00 · 06:00 · 12:00 rather than
06:00 · 00:00 · 18:00, and each one that opens a new UTC day prints the date under it. The plot's
height grows by 12px when it has dated ticks, so nothing is clipped.

**The range deltas**: `cumulative` already knew the level at the window's start (`baseUpper`,
`baseLower`); the legend now prints `total − base` beside each total. Making that number *true*
needed one more fix: buckets are floored to their own step so ticks land on clock boundaries, and
that floor reaches back up to a whole step before "seven days ago", which quietly pulled in a work
log finished ten hours before the window opened. The first bucket is now clipped back to the
requested start (except when the window was widened to keep a young project's chart from being
three marks wide), so the legend's `+3 finished` is the vault's 3.

**The feed** opens days until it reaches back past 24 hours *and* has ten rows, capped at three
days, so a manager returning to ten hours of silence gets yesterday too instead of one open day
and twenty-one closed drawers.

## Outcome

Shipped. The dashboard now answers "what is going on, and what needs me" in words a reader does
not have to infer, and the two charts label the period they cover.

**What is on the screen that was not there before.**

- *Open bugs, by name.* One row per open bug — status dot, title, severity pill, id, the
  assignee's avatar or an `unclaimed` chip, "open for 13h 2m", and "untouched" when nothing has
  happened since it was filed — sorted worst-first with the unowned ahead of the owned, three
  rows and then a link to the board. On relay that puts `BUG-0008 · High · unclaimed · open for
  13h 2m · untouched` at the top of the panel, where round 1 printed four anonymous severity
  chips and named only the *oldest* bug, which is the medium one somebody was already holding.
- *The newest note of every in-flight work log,* quoted as the opening sentence of each of its
  paragraphs — the first two and the last when there are more, with the skip marked by a leading
  `…`. This is how "Pause is wired and works, and I have found a problem I cannot solve on my
  side" reaches the dashboard: the event log's summary stops after the first clause, so the strip
  reads the record itself for the (at most three) logs that are open.
- *Liveness measured from the clock.* LIVE means something was recorded in the last two hours.
  An open log nobody has touched for hours carries a hollow ring and "no update in 10h 47m"
  rather than a live blue dot; one with no notes at all says "no updates yet" instead of dressing
  its own start time up as an update. The Agents table's dots follow the same rule.
- *Dated axes.* Ticks land on natural boundaries and every tick that opens a new UTC day prints
  the date beneath it, so 7 days reads `12 Aug · 13 Aug · … · 18 Aug` (BUG-0011).
- *A range control that moves numbers.* Each legend entry carries the change inside the window —
  `+3`, `−1`, `±0` — beside its running total, and the first bucket is clipped back to the
  window's start so those figures are the vault's figures (BUG-0012).
- *UTC on the face of the page* — in the sentence under the range control, in the Activity card's
  count, and under the 24-hour bars — instead of only inside `title` attributes.
- *A feed that opens on time, not on volume:* days open until the feed reaches back past 24 hours
  and holds ten rows, capped at three days, so ten hours of silence no longer greets a reader
  with one open day and twenty-one closed drawers. Its collapsed day rows are as long as the day
  was busy, carry a legend for their five tones, and draw the day's cast as avatars rather than a
  list of names cut mid-name.
- *A 24-hour panel that is a chart:* a full-width band of hourly bars with named hours, the
  current hour in the accent, and "3 events in the busiest hour · times UTC" under it.

**Not done, deliberately.** The in-flight rows say what the newest note says; they do not label a
record "blocked". Nothing in the vault's schema records a block, and inferring one from prose
would be the dashboard putting a word in an agent's mouth. Quoting the sentence the agent wrote
carries the same fact and stays true when the guess would have been wrong.
