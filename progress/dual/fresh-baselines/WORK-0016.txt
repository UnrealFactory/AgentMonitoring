Explain it like I'm five
The night the app got ready to ship
One work session, three big jobs: teach every screen the same words , give the desktop app a guard dog , and box it up for the world.
1
Before: one thing, five names
Two tasks were being worked on. Every screen agreed on the fact — but each screen made up its own word for it. Like a family where everyone calls the same dog a different name.
"active"
"still open"
"in flight"
"work open"
"in progress"
Same dog. Five names. Very confusing.
The fix: one shared word list (a file called words.ts ) that every screen must read from . Now the dog has one name, everywhere.
2
Numbers now tell the whole story
"2" alone means nothing. 2 out of what — 3? 200? Every count now brings its "out of" with it.
BEFORE
2
…two of what?
AFTER
"2 of 12 in progress"
Every screen — the switcher, the dashboard, the list, the charts — now says "2 of 12" , never just "2".
3
Twins in search get name tags
Two projects can both have a task numbered 0002. Searching used to show two identical twins. Now each one wears a badge saying which family it's from.
WORK-0002
quill
WORK-0002
AgentMonitoring
Same number, different badge — you can finally tell them apart at a glance.

The desktop app gets a guard dog
The app watches a folder of data (the "vault"). Here's the sneaky part: if that folder disappears, the watcher doesn't scream — it just goes silent . The app would keep showing old numbers forever, looking perfectly fine.

And it was proved for real : they ran the finished app, yanked the data file away — banner appeared in 13 seconds. Put it back — banner gone, new updates flowed in. Not a promise. A test.
