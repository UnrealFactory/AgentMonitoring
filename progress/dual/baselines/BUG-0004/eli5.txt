The screen that rewrote the notebook
One page in the app has one job: show a saved note exactly as written. It didn’t. It erased a number and made up a new one.
1
What the notebook really says
A long sentence wraps onto a second line. The second line happens to start with “ 200. ” — the end of the sentence, not a list.

200.

“…returns 503 for three hours, then 200 .” — the 200 is just the next word of the sentence.
2
What the screen showed instead
The app saw a line starting with “number + dot” and thought: “Ooh, a numbered list!” So it threw the 200 away and stamped its own “1.” there.

The lie: 200 is gone, and a “1.” that nobody wrote appears.
The page deleted a real number and printed a fake one — and looked perfectly normal doing it.
3
Why? The app used one dumb rule
Its whole test for “is this a list?” was: does the line start with a number and a dot? That catches real lists… and also any wrapped sentence that lands on a number.
“Number + dot? LIST!”
1. buy milk
2. buy eggs
✅
a real list — fine
…hours, then
200. Without jitter…

a wrapped sentence — mangled
Same stamp for both. It can’t tell a shopping list from the middle of a sentence.
4
The fix: a smarter rule
The Markdown standard already solved this exact problem: mid-paragraph, only a line starting with “ 1. ” may begin a numbered list. “200.”? “503.”? “2026.”? Those stay in the sentence.
