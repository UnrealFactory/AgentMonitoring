The owner settled two things in two sentences. Archive, the item that hid a project from the lists without removing it, will never be used — take it out. And a project they have stopped working on should be removable from the app they read it in.

**Archive is gone and Delete is in.** Every trace of it left the menus, the screens and both languages' wording. A red Delete now sits on every surface that shows a project.

**It will not act until you type the project's short name.** `src/components/DeleteProject.tsx` is the one confirmation window. It names the project, counts what goes with it, and says plainly that its folder leaves your disk. Agents cannot do this at all: `agentmon`, the command-line tool agents write records with, has no verb for it.

**The dangerous part is not the button, it is the aim.** Every project is a folder, and all of them sit under one projects folder. Before removing anything, `Vault::delete_project` resolves the folder to where it really is on disk. `inside_projects` then demands that it still sit directly under that same projects folder.

It is like reading the address on the parcel again once you are at the door.

**A later round caught worse.** A reviewing agent parked one window inside a project and deleted it from a second window. The parked window, on the work list, kept printing all twelve rows. Its own sidebar had already dropped that project: one window contradicting itself. The dashboard flew its live badge over a folder that was not on the disk.

`projectGone` now turns those screens into the app's own "no such project" screen.

**Then the last copy nearly went too.** A record whose project has just been deleted keeps what the app already read, under `StaleRecordBar`, a bar saying so. Beside that bar sat a Try again button, and one click threw that last copy away. Its only button now is the way out.

**`npm run check:keys` now parks a window inside a project before deleting it.** That run drives the app by keyboard and reads what lands on screen. It grew from 158 checks to 223 in each language. A person also walked the flow in the real desktop window, and nothing was deleted outside temporary copies.

A delete nothing can reverse should cost you the effort of typing the name.
