The list of bugs gave each bug two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was. You could narrow it by that dot or by a label, and by nothing else. A bug's own page gave the report, the conversation and the fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** Three tabs run across the top: Open, Resolved, All. Each carries a count that already obeys your other choices, so it answers "how many will I see if I switch?" You can also narrow by how bad a bug is, by label, by who owns it, by who filed it, and by typing.

**Each bug is now one line.** It shows how bad it is as a coloured tag, then its number, its title, its labels, who filed it, who owns it, its comments and its age. Open bugs sit under their own heading, worst first.

**An empty board says which kind of empty it is.** Nothing filed, nothing still open, nothing matching what you typed: three separate pages, each offering the command or the button that answers it.

**A bug's own page now has a moment.** One strip holds the three dates, and they appear nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed. It shows the real gaps between them, and draws a dashed leg for what has not happened yet. Being claimed is its own entry in the conversation — "nova claimed this bug · 35m after it was filed". The fix ends the page as a wide coloured banner: who fixed it, and how long it had been open.

**Three faults in shared code went first; both pages would have inherited them.** The code that draws a record read any line that opened with a number and a full stop. It treated that line as the start of a numbered list. So a sentence wrapping onto "200. Without jitter…" lost the 200 and printed "1." — that is `BUG-0004`, filed and fixed here.

It is like a typist who sees a figure at the top of a line and decides you meant to begin a list.

**A row too narrow for everything in it used to throw the title away first.** It sheds its tag chips first now, and the title stays. And a record listed only the records it points at; each now lists the ones pointing back too.

**`npm run smoke` is the check that would have caught this.** It draws nine passages copied word for word out of real records. It then reads every record the app was holding. It fails if one word of a stored record is missing from the drawn page. All 58 of its checks pass.

**I made it fail on purpose before trusting it.** Putting the old behaviour back made the check fail, with the 200 gone from the drawn text.

**I drove the finished board in a real browser, not a picture of it.** On this project's own bugs the tabs read 2, 6 and 8. Searching "signature" left one row. A nonsense search gave the designed empty page, and its button brought all eight back. Nothing errored.

Still wrong: the tabs over the work-log list count the whole project while the rows beneath are filtered — `BUG-0005`, left for another round. The board has no sort control, so its order is written out in words under the list. And a list inside a list still comes out flat; no record uses one today.

A list you cannot narrow is a list somebody has to read from the top.
