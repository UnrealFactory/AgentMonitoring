A work record in another project this app was tracking described a receiving machine. It answered 503 for three hours, then 200 — and 200 meant it had recovered. On the page the 200 was gone. A "1." stood in its place, and one sentence had been broken into three pieces. Nothing looked wrong, on the one screen whose whole job is to show a record exactly as it was written.

**A reviewer got the number back only by luck.** That reviewer reads a record cold and says whether a newcomer could follow it. The phrase "503 for three hours then 200" happened to be written a second time, further down. That second copy is where it came back from.

**A sentence too long for one line had wrapped, and the next line began "200.".** The code that turns a stored record into a page used one test for two questions. Does a numbered list start on this line? Does the paragraph I am in end here? A line opening with a number and a full stop answers yes to the first, and should almost never answer yes to the second.

It is like a form where anything on a numbered line gets renumbered, so your "200" comes back as "1".

**A public rulebook already said so.** CommonMark is the agreed standard for the marks writers put into plain text — the stars, the dashes, the back-quotes. Its rule is that a numbered list may cut into a running paragraph only when it starts at 1. That rule exists for exactly this reason. I split the text-reading part out of the page-drawing part and taught it that rule.

**Two nearby faults went with it.** A list the writer began at 3 keeps those numbers now. And bold text is read all the way through at last. A name marked as code inside bold arrives as a name, not as quote marks.

**`npm run smoke` is the check that would have caught this.** It draws nine passages copied word for word out of real records, this sentence among them. Then it reads every record of both projects the app held then: twenty in one, ten in the other. It fails if a word of a stored record is missing from the drawn page. All 55 of its checks pass.

**I made it fail on purpose before trusting it.** Putting the old behaviour back failed two of the nine passages, and printed the drawn text with the 200 missing. Restoring the rule passed them again.

**Lists inside lists still come out flat.** No record uses one today; I searched for them. Doing that properly is a bigger rebuild than this fix was.

The worst wrong page is the one that looks fine.
