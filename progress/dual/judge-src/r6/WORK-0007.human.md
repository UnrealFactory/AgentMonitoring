The list of bugs gave each bug two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was. You could narrow it by that dot or by a label, and by nothing else. A bug's own page gave the report, the conversation and the fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** `src/pages/BugsPage.tsx`, the page listing a project's bugs, runs three tabs: Open, Resolved, All. Each tab carries a count that already obeys your other choices, so it answers "how many will I see if I switch?" You can also narrow it by how bad a bug is, by label, by owner, by who filed it, and by typing.

**Each bug is now one line.** It shows how bad it is as a coloured tag, then its number and title. Then its labels, who filed it, who owns it, its comments and its age. Open bugs sit under their own heading, worst first.

**An empty board says which kind of empty it is.** Nothing filed, nothing still open, nothing matching what you typed: three separate pages. Each offers the command or the button that answers it.

**A bug's own page now has a moment.** `src/pages/BugDetailPage.tsx` puts the three dates in one strip, and nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed. It shows the real gaps between them, and draws a dashed leg for what has not happened yet. Being claimed is its own entry in the conversation — "nova claimed this bug · 35m after it was filed". The fix ends the page as a wide coloured banner: who fixed it, and how long it had been open.

**Three faults in shared code went first; both pages would have inherited them.** `src/lib/markdown.tsx` turned a stored record into the page you read. It read any line opening with a number and a full stop as the start of a numbered list. So a sentence in `WORK-0005` that wrapped onto "200. Without jitter…" lost the 200 and printed "1." — that is `BUG-0004`, filed and fixed here.

It is like a typist who sees a figure at the top of a line and decides you meant to begin a list.

**A row too narrow for everything in it used to throw the title away first.** `.work-rows`, the box both lists draw their rows in, sheds its tag chips first now, and the title stays. And a record listed only the records it points at; `src/components/Related.tsx` now adds the ones pointing back.

**`npm run smoke` is the check that would have caught this.** It draws nine passages copied word for word out of real records. It then reads every record in `./vault`, the folder the app read its records out of. It fails if one word of a stored record is missing from the drawn page. All 58 of its checks pass.

**I made it fail on purpose before trusting it.** Putting the old behaviour back made the check fail, with the 200 gone from the drawn text.

**I drove the finished board in a real browser, then read the screens it drew.** Playwright does the clicking, the way a person would, and it ran against those same stored records.

The tabs came back 2, 6 and 8. Searching "signature" left one row. A nonsense search gave the designed empty page, and its button brought all eight back. Nothing errored.

Still wrong: the tabs over the work-log list still count the whole project — `BUG-0005`, left for another round. The board has no sort control, so its order is written out in words under the list. And a list inside a list still comes out flat; no record uses one today.
