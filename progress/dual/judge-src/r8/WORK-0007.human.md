The list of bugs gave each bug two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was, and nothing to narrow it by but that dot and a label. A bug's own page gave the report, the conversation and the fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** `src/pages/BugsPage.tsx`, the page listing a project's bugs, runs three tabs: Open, Resolved, All. Each count already obeys your other choices, so it answers "how many will I see if I switch?" You can narrow it by how bad a bug is, by label, by owner, by who filed it, and by typing.

**A bug's own page has a moment now.** It puts the three dates in one strip and nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed, with the real gaps between them. The fix ends the page as a wide coloured banner naming who fixed it and how long it had been open.

**Three faults in shared code went first; both pages would have inherited them.** `src/lib/markdown.tsx` turned a stored record into the page you read. It read any line opening with a number and a full stop as a numbered list. So a stored sentence that wrapped onto "200. Without jitter…" lost the 200 and printed "1." — that is `BUG-0004`, filed and fixed here.

It is like a typist who sees a figure starting a line and decides you meant a list.

**The other two faults were in the rows and in the links.** A row too narrow for its contents used to throw the title away first; it sheds its tag chips first now. And a record listed only the records it points at; it shows the ones pointing back too.

**`npm run smoke` is the check that would have caught this.** It draws nine passages copied word for word out of real records, then reads every record in `./vault` — the folder the app read its records out of — and fails if a word of one goes missing from the drawn page. All 58 of its checks pass. I made it fail on purpose first: putting the old behaviour back lost the 200 from the drawn text.

**I drove the finished board in a real browser, then read the screens it drew.** Playwright did the clicking, the way a person would, against those same stored records. The tabs came back 2, 6 and 8. Searching "signature" left one row. A nonsense search gave the designed empty page, whose button brought all eight back.

Still wrong: the tabs over the work-log list still count the whole project, filed for another round.

A list you cannot narrow is a list somebody has to read from the top.
