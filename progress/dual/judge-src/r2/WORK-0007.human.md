The list of bugs gave each bug two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was. You could narrow it by that dot or by a label, and by nothing else. A bug's own page gave report, conversation and fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** Three tabs run across the top — Open, Resolved, All — each carrying a count that already obeys your other choices, so the number answers "how many will I see if I switch". You can also narrow by how bad a bug is, by label, by who owns it, by who filed it, and by typing. Each bug is then one line: a coloured tag for how bad it is, its number, title, labels, who filed and who owns it, comments, age. Open bugs sit under a heading of their own, worst first.

**An empty board says which kind of empty it is.** Nothing filed, nothing still open, nothing matching what you typed: three separate pages, each offering the command or button that answers it.

**A bug's own page now has a moment.** A strip across the top holds the three dates and they appear nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed, with the real gaps between and a dashed leg for what has not happened. Being claimed shows up in the conversation as its own entry — "nova claimed this bug · 35m after it was filed". The fix ends the page as a wide coloured banner: who fixed it, and how long it had been open.

**Three faults in shared code went first; both pages would have inherited them.** The code that draws a record read any line opening with a number and a full stop as the start of a numbered list, so a sentence wrapping onto "200. Without jitter…" lost the 200 and printed "1." — that is `BUG-0004`, filed and fixed here. A row running out of width used to sacrifice its title first, and sheds its tag chips first now. And a record listed only the records it points at; each now lists the ones pointing back.

It is like a typist who sees a figure at the top of a line and decides you meant to begin a list.

**I drove the finished board rather than looking at a picture of it.** In a real browser, on this project's own bugs, the tabs read 2, 6 and 8; searching "signature" left one row; a nonsense search gave the designed empty page, whose button brought all eight back. Nothing errored.

Still wrong: the tabs over the work-log list count the whole project while the rows beneath are filtered — `BUG-0005`, for another round. The board has no sort control; the order it uses is written in words beneath the list.

A list you cannot narrow is a list somebody has to read from the top.
