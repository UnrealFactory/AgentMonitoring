The list of bugs gave each one two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was, and nothing to narrow it by but that dot or a label. A bug's page gave report, conversation and fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** Three tabs, Open, Resolved and All, carry counts that obey your other choices, so each answers "how many will I see if I switch?" It narrows by how bad a bug is, by label, by owner, by who filed it, and by typing.

**Each bug is one line:** how bad it is as a coloured tag, its number and title, labels, who filed it, who owns it, comments and age. Open bugs sit under their own heading, worst first.

**An empty board says which kind of empty it is.** Nothing filed, nothing still open, nothing matching what you typed: three pages, each with the command or button that answers it.

**A bug's page has a moment now.** The three dates sit in one strip and nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed, with the real gaps between. Being claimed is an entry in the conversation: "nova claimed this bug · 35m after it was filed". The fix ends the page as a coloured banner: who fixed it, and how long it had been open.

**A row too narrow for its contents used to throw the title away first; now it sheds its tag chips.** It measures itself, not the window. But the narrow-row rule weighs as much as the plain rule setting each column's width, and rules of equal weight are settled by whichever was written last. So it must sit below all of them, or it quietly loses.

It is like two orders of equal rank: the room does what the later one said.

**I drove the finished board in a real browser and read the screens it drew.** Playwright did the clicking, the way a person would, on real stored records. The tabs came back 2, 6 and 8. A nonsense search gave the designed empty page, whose button brought all eight back.

**`npm run smoke` would have caught `BUG-0004`, the fault fixed here where numbers inside a stored record were silently rewritten.** It fails if a word of any stored record is missing from the drawn page, and all 58 of its checks pass.

Still wrong: the work-log list's tabs count the whole project, `BUG-0005`, filed for another round. The board has no sort control, so its order is written out under the list. A list inside a list comes out flat, and no record uses one.

A list you cannot narrow is a list somebody has to read from the top.
