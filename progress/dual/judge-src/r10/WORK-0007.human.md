The list of bugs gave each bug two lines and a dot coloured for how bad it was. You could narrow it by that dot or by a label, and by nothing else. A bug's own page gave the report, the conversation and the fix equal weight.

**The board is a triage screen now.** `src/pages/BugsPage.tsx`, the page that lists a project's bugs, runs three tabs: Open, Resolved and All. Each tab carries a count that already obeys your other choices, so it answers "how many will I see if I switch?" You can also narrow the list by how bad a bug is, by label, by owner, by who filed it, and by typing.

**Each bug is now one line.** It shows how bad it is as a coloured tag, then its number and title. Then its labels, who filed it, who owns it, its comments and its age. Open bugs sit under their own heading, worst first.

**An empty board says which kind of empty it is.** Nothing filed, nothing still open, nothing matching what you typed: three separate pages. Each one offers the command or the button that answers it.

**A bug's own page now has a moment.** `src/pages/BugDetailPage.tsx` puts the three dates in one strip, and nowhere else: filed, claimed, fixed. It shows the real gaps between them, and draws a dashed leg for what has not happened yet. Being claimed is its own entry in the conversation — "nova claimed this bug · 35m after it was filed". The fix ends the page as a coloured banner: who fixed it, when, and how long it had been open.

**I drove the finished board in a real browser, then read the screens it drew.** Playwright does the clicking, the way a person would, and it ran against real stored records.

The tabs came back 2, 6 and 8. Searching "signature" left one row. A nonsense search gave the designed empty page, and its button brought all eight back. Nothing errored.

**Three faults sat in the code the bug pages and the work pages both run on.** Fixing them there fixed them for both.

**The first fault ate numbers.** `src/lib/markdown.tsx` turned a stored record into the page you read. It treated any line opening with a number and a full stop as the start of a numbered list. A sentence inside `WORK-0005`, one of the stored work records, wrapped onto a second line beginning "200." The drawn page dropped that 200 and printed "1." in its place. That is `BUG-0004`, filed and fixed here.

It is like a typist who sees a figure at the top of a line and decides you meant to begin a list.

What was stored never changed. The drawing of it was wrong, and the drawing is what I repaired.

**`npm run smoke` is the check that would have caught this.** It draws nine passages copied word for word out of real records, that wrapped sentence among them. It then reads every record in `./vault`, the folder the app read its records out of. It fails if one word of a stored record is missing from the drawn page. All 58 of its checks pass.

**I made it fail on purpose before trusting it.** Putting the old behaviour back made the check fail, with the 200 gone from the drawn text.

**The second fault threw away the title.** A row too narrow for everything in it used to sacrifice its title first. `.work-rows`, the box both lists draw their rows in, now sheds its tag chips first and keeps the title. It measures its own width rather than the window's.

**That new rule nearly lost to the ones it had to beat.** It weighs exactly as much as the plain rules setting each column's width. Two rules of equal weight are settled by whichever was written last. So it has to sit below all of them, or it quietly loses.

**At the size the app opens on, one title in twelve is now cut short.** I counted the titles too wide for their own row rather than judging by eye. Even that one title is 93% there. No row spilled outside its box at any width I tried.

**The third fault made every link one-way.** A record's page listed the records it points at, and nothing showed the ones pointing back at it. `src/components/Related.tsx`, one block both kinds of record page now draw, adds them. That same work record now shows on its own page that its follow-ups became three bugs.

Still wrong: the tabs over the work-log list still count the whole project — `BUG-0005`, left for another round. The board has no sort control, so its order is written out in words under the list. And a list inside a list still comes out flat; no record uses one today.

A list you cannot narrow is a list somebody has to read from the top.
