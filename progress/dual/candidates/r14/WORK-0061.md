ElmwoodOnline, another project that uses this app, keeps one note that maps its other notes. Every row of that map names a note inside double square brackets — `[[unrealnetcore-queue]]` is one. Agents write names that way out of habit. Our app printed the rows exactly as typed, and nothing on them could be clicked.

**Each row is now a chip — a name you click to open the note it names.** I added the double-bracket spelling to the rules that turn written text into what you see. A bracketed name becomes the same sort of link the app already makes for a record number.

**A name without brackets still stays plain words.** Note names are lowercase words joined by hyphens, and ordinary sentences carry such words too. Linking every one of them would turn plain phrases into links by accident. The brackets are how a writer says "I mean the note." A record number inside brackets works too.

It is like quotation marks round a word: not part of the word, just a sign you meant it as a name.

**An ordinary link can open with a bracket too.** A link puts its visible label in square brackets before its address. Give that label a name starting with a bracket and the line begins with two, just like a note reference. So the app matches the whole shape, and those links stay links.

**The costlier trap was the trip back.** The app also has to turn formatted text back into plain writing. My first attempt threw the brackets away on that return trip. In Elmwood's real notes, Korean grammar particles sit welded to the closing brackets, as in `unrealnetcore-queue]]의`. Dropping the brackets would have eaten letters out of someone's sentence.

**Three checks caught that while I worked, and they pass now.** `node scripts/markdown-smoke.mjs` feeds written text through the display code and compares each result with what we wrote down it should be. A display rule that quietly rewrites people's words hands a reader a sentence its writer never typed. That script makes 106 checks over Elmwood's own folder, those three among them, and 443 over ours, with a new group for this case.

**I opened the Elmwood map in the app and looked.** Every row of it shows as a chip, and the record-number chips are unchanged.

**The tooltip and the right-click menu came free.** Chips already worked that way for record numbers. Forget the brackets and nothing links, which is on purpose. Left out: two documents that now write the rule down. Neither changes what you click.

Better to link what a writer marked than to guess at what they meant.
