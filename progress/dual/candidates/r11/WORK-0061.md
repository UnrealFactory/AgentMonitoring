The assistants working here leave written notes for each other. Inside a note they point at another by its name in double brackets — `[[unrealnetcore-queue]]`. ElmwoodOnline's UnrealNetCore, a neighbouring project, keeps an index note whose rows are all pointers like that. Our app printed each row as flat text, brackets and all. None of them was a link.

**Record ids already linked; note names never did.** An id like `WORK-0004` has long become a small clickable label that opens that record. A note's name never did, on purpose: note names are ordinary hyphenated words, and we did not want every hyphenated word becoming a link. The two brackets are the writer saying "I mean the note".

It is like underlining a word to show you meant it as a name, rather than leaving the reader to guess.

**I taught the text reader one new pattern.** `src/lib/markdown-parse.ts` is the file that reads our written text and decides which parts of a line are links. I added `WIKILINK_RE`, a pattern that spots a name inside double brackets. It accepts only the shape a note name may have: 2 to 64 letters, digits or hyphens, no hyphen at either end. The link it builds is the one the ids already build.

**Turning a line back into plain text ate the brackets.** `inlineText`, the part that flattens a marked-up line back into plain words, dropped the brackets on both sides. Korean sticks its particles — small grammar words — onto the end of the word before them. In these notes they sit right against the closing brackets: `unrealnetcore-queue]]의`. Eat those brackets and part of what someone wrote is gone. Three checks caught it while I worked, and flattening now puts them back.

**The check script passes 443 and 106.** `node scripts/markdown-smoke.mjs` feeds prepared scraps of text through this reader and compares each result with the answer we wrote down. All 443 of its checks pass, including a new group for bracketed names in prose and in table cells. Pointed at Elmwood's real folder with `--dir C:/Code/ElmwoodOnline/AgentMonitoring`, it passes 106, the three that had broken among them.

**I opened the Elmwood note myself and looked.** In the browser I loaded that live index note and saved `progress/shots-wikilink/note-detail-full.png`. Every bracketed row now shows as a note link, in prose and in table cells alike. The record links look as they did before.

**A hyphenated word with no brackets still never links.** The rule is now written down twice. `SPEC.md` states how the app must behave, and `docs/AGENT_MANUAL.md` is the instructions the assistants read. Ids link, bracketed note names link, plain hyphenated words never do.

Two brackets are cheap; guessing what someone meant is not.
