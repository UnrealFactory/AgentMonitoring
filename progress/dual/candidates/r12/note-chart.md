The activity charts count several kinds of work side by side, and each kind gets its own colour so a reader can tell them apart at a glance. Notes needed one of their own. We gave them a pink, `--series-note: #c9629b`, for the note series — the part of those charts that counts notes.

Before settling on it I ran the pink through the dataviz palette validator, the tool that checks a set of chart colours stay clearly different from one another. When a check of that kind comes back no, two of the things being counted look alike, and whoever reads the chart cannot tell which is which. I ran the pink against the three colours it actually appears with today: the work, done and bug series.

It passed against those three, and those three are all it passed against. It was never held up next to fix-purple, the colour standing for fixes, or against the greys.

It is like trying out a new voice against the people already in the room. Someone else walks in later, and nobody has listened.

So the charts we draw today are safe to read. If anyone builds a new chart that sets `#c9629b` beside fix-purple or a grey, that pairing has never been looked at — run the validator on it first.

A colour is only proven against the colours it was shown beside.
