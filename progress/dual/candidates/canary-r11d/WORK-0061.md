One of our live projects keeps a note that works as a map of its other notes. Each row names the note it points at, inside double square brackets — `[[unrealnetcore-queue]]` and rows like it. On screen those rows sat there as plain text, brackets and all, with nothing to click. Record numbers written in the same prose already become tags you can click.

**Agents type the brackets whether we read them or not.** It is the spelling they reach for from their note-taking apps. So I taught `src/lib/markdown-parse.ts`, the file that turns a record's text into what you see on screen, to read a bracketed note name and give it the same tag a record number gets.

**A hyphenated word on its own still stays dead text.** Note names look like ordinary words with hyphens, and linking every such word would be wrong more often than right. The brackets are how a writer says: I mean the note. It is like a raised hand in a room — the app answers whoever raises one.

**The quick way to spot them would have broken ordinary links.** Checking that a piece of text opens with two brackets is not enough. An ordinary web link can open that way too, when the words it displays begin with a bracket. So the app measures the whole name first: two to sixty-four letters, digits or hyphens, and no hyphen at either end.

**Turning a tag back into plain text nearly rewrote what people wrote.** The app can also flatten a tag back into ordinary text, and mid-work it handed the name back with its brackets gone. In real notes the next Korean word runs straight onto the closing brackets, with no space — `unrealnetcore-queue]]의`. Dropping the brackets there changes somebody's sentence. Putting them back fixed it.

**The same check ran twice, and then I looked.** `node scripts/markdown-smoke.mjs` — the script that runs prepared scraps of record text through the reader and checks each result against what we wrote down — passes all 443 of its checks, with new ones for brackets in prose and in table cells, and for the ordinary link that must not be mistaken for one. Pointed at that live project's own records, the same script passes 106, including the three that failed while the brackets were being dropped. Then I opened the map note in the app: every row is a tag now, and the record tags are unchanged.

**Anyone reading that map can now click straight through to the note a row names.** Leave a name bare, without brackets, and it stays a word on the page, exactly as before. The rule now sits in `docs/AGENT_MANUAL.md`, the handbook agents read before they work, and in our spec: bracket it, or it does not link.

The spelling your people already type is the one worth teaching the machine.
