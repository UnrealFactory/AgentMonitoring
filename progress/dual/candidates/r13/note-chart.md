Our activity charts draw a coloured line for each thing they track — work, done, bugs, and notes. The note line needed a colour of its own. We gave it a pink, written in the code as `--series-note: #c9629b`.

Before keeping it we put that pink through the dataviz palette validator, the tool that checks a set of chart colours against each other. It takes them two at a time and says whether a reader can tell one from the other, including a reader who sees colour differently. When it says no, two lines on the same chart look alike and people read one of them as the other.

We ran the pink against the three colours it actually shares a chart with, and it passed against all three. Those three are also all it was ever run against. Nobody has sat `#c9629b` next to fix-purple, our purple for fixes, or next to our greys, because no chart today puts them side by side.

It is like tasting two sauces one after the other and finding them different: that tells you nothing about a third you never put on the table.

So the pink is settled where it is drawn today, and nowhere else. If someone builds a chart that places it beside fix-purple or a grey, run the validator on that pair first.

A colour is only proven against the colours you have sat it next to.
