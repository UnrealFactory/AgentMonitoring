The activity charts, the graphs showing what agents did over time, draw one line per kind of
activity. The note line stands for agent notes, and it needed a colour of its own. The record does
not say that anything was broken first, and it names nobody who was hurt.

**What we picked.** The note line is now a pink, written as `#c9629b`, which is the way a program
spells one exact shade, and stored under the label `--series-note`, the name the charts reach for
whenever they draw that line.

**How we checked it.** We ran the pink through the palette validator, a tool that takes a set of
chart colours and tests whether a reader can still tell them apart. We ran it against three colours
only: the work, done and bug lines, which are the lines the note line actually appears beside today.

Think of it as trying a new voice against the three people already in the room, rather than against
everyone who might walk in later.

Two colours were kept out of that check on purpose: fix-purple, the colour another line uses, and
the grey scale, the range of greys the charts use for their quieter parts. The record does not say
why those two were left out, and it does not name a colour we considered and turned down.

The evidence here is thin, and the record admits it by what it omits. It says the pink passed the
palette validator, but gives no number, and does not say what the validator measures or what it
would have caught.

So the note line has a colour, and a standing limit rides with it: any new chart that puts the pink
beside fix-purple or the greys must run the palette validator again first. A colour is only proven
against the colours it was tested with.
