Drag the desktop window narrow and the Korean bug board stopped printing words in the column saying how bad a bug is. Each row showed one syllable: 치, 높, 보, 낮. Three are not Korean words. The fourth is: 낮 means daytime, printed in the rows meant to say a bug is not urgent. A reader dragging a window edge found it.

**The app was cutting each word down to its first character.** In English that gives C, H, M and L, initials a reader unpacks back into the word; a Korean word's first syllable unpacks into nothing. It is like keeping the first note of a tune and expecting people to hum the rest. The short form is an entry in each language's word list now — `word.sevAbbr`, C/H/M/L in English, nothing in Korean — and where it is empty none is drawn.

**The saving it was made for does not exist in Korean.** A comment in the code defended the cut: the letter costs seven pixels and keeps the meaning. That was argued in English and never re-asked in Korean. Measured here, every Korean word in that column is narrower than the English it replaces — the widest 16 pixels under Medium, the word the cut-off was sized to fit.

**Two checks were blind by build.** `npm run check:i18n` opens the app's screens and reports wording left wrong for its reader; without one, a screen ships unreadable to the reader it was written for. It read one wide window, and this narrow layout only appears below a threshold, so nothing had seen it; it reads the desktop's narrowest width now.

**Asking which language a string is in cannot tell a word from a piece of one.** 낮 is Korean either way. The same check now demands the three word lists the app writes itself print an entry exactly, and refuses a Korean short form outright.

**Putting the fault back makes them fail.** The first-character rule restored: 30 findings, all on that board at the width nothing used to visit. The syllable 낮 typed in as a short form: refused by the second check, the case the first cannot see. In the real desktop window at its narrowest, the row reads 낮음 under a legend reading 낮음.

**The same English habit had broken a date one surface over.** A rule letting a line break at any character split `2026년 8월 18` from a lone `일` beneath it at eleven widths, every one between the two the check was reading. That rule is gone. 일 counts days and cannot stand apart from its number.

A short form only works where the reader knows how to make it long again.
